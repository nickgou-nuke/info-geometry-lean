## 1. Rosser-Schoenfeld Prime Count Bound (`GenuineBounds.lean`)

### Target
`rosser_schoenfeld_prime_count_bound (x : ℕ) (hx : x ≥ 55)` in [GenuineBounds.lean](file:///home/goutev/repos/info-geometry-lean/lean/InfoGeometry/Arithmetic/GenuineBounds.lean#L209)

### Mathematical Statement
$$ \left| \pi(x) - \text{li}(x) \right| < \frac{x}{8 \pi \sqrt{x} \log x} \quad \text{for } x \ge 55 $$

### Chain of Thought & Proof Architecture
1. **Explicit Zero-Free Region for $\zeta(s)$**:
   The derivation of explicit (non-asymptotic) error bounds for the prime counting function $\pi(x)$ relies on the explicit zero-free region of the Riemann Zeta function. The classical result of Rosser and Schoenfeld (1962) establishes that $\zeta(s)$ has no zeros in the region:
   $$ \sigma \ge 1 - \frac{1}{R \log |t|} \quad \text{for } |t| \ge 2, \text{ where } R = 9.6459 $$
2. **Explicit Formula for Chebyshev's $\psi(x)$**:
   We relate $\psi(x) = \sum_{p^m \le x} \log p$ to the zeros $\rho = \beta + i\gamma$ of $\zeta(s)$ via the explicit formula:
   $$ \psi(x) = x - \sum_{|\gamma| < T} \frac{x^\rho}{\rho} - \log(2\pi) - \frac{1}{2}\log(1 - x^{-2}) - R(x, T) $$
   By choosing $T$ optimally as a function of $x$, the sum over the zeros can be bounded explicitly using the zero-free region:
   $$ |\psi(x) - x| < \varepsilon(x) x $$
3. **Integration by Parts (Stieltjes Integral)**:
   We recover $\pi(x)$ from $\theta(x) = \sum_{p \le x} \log p$ (and hence $\psi(x)$) using Stieltjes integration:
   $$ \pi(x) = \frac{\theta(x)}{\log x} + \int_2^x \frac{\theta(t)}{t \log^2 t} dt $$
   Substituting the explicit error bounds for $\theta(t)$ into the integral yields the desired bound:
   $$ |\pi(x) - \text{li}(x)| < \frac{x}{8\pi \sqrt{x} \log x} $$

There exists a conformal spinor package $(K, B, J, \chi, \varepsilon, \rho)$ such that the following diagram commutes for all imaginary split-octonions $X \in \text{Imaginary}$:
$$ P_K \circ \rho(\text{incl}_{4\to 5}(\iota(e(X)))) \circ \iota_K = e \circ L_X \circ e^{-1} $$
where $L_X$ is left multiplication by $X$ on `CanonicalZorn`.

### Chain of Thought & Proof Architecture
1. **Dimension Matching & Linear Equivalence**:

   The Bott inclusion map `incl_Cl_split 4` embeds $Cl(4,4)$ into $Cl(5,5)$ by mapping the generators $e_1, \dots, e_8$ to the corresponding generators in $Cl(5,5)$.
   The spinor representation $\rho$ maps a vector in $SplitSpace 5$ to an endomorphism of $K$ via the split gamma matrices:
   $$ \rho(\text{incl}(v)) = \gamma_1 v_1 + \dots + \gamma_8 v_8 $$
3. **Index Retraction and Triality**:
   The projection $P_K : K \to \text{SplitSpace 4}$ and injection $\iota_K : \text{SplitSpace 4} \to K$ are concrete coordinate-matching maps. Under this projection, the action of the $Cl(5,5)$ gamma matrices $\gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_8$ restricted to the 8-dimensional subspace matches exactly the left-multiplication action of the split-octonions $L_X$ on `CanonicalZorn` under the equivalence $e$.

## 3. Operator Roots in the Subalgebra (`GoldenMeanShift.lean`)

### Target
`X_root (m : ℕ) (Y : CuntzAlg 2) (h_comm : Y * X = X * Y) (h_root : Y ^ m = X)` in [GoldenMeanShift.lean](file:///home/goutev/repos/info-geometry-lean/lean/sandbox/GoldenMeanShift.lean#L112)

### Mathematical Statement
Any $Y \in \mathcal{O}_2$ that commutes with $X$ and satisfies $Y^m = X$ is a linear combination of $1$ and $X$.

### Chain of Thought & Proof Architecture
1. **Subalgebra Structure**:
   Let $\mathcal{A} = \text{adjoin } \mathbb{C} \{X\}$ be the subalgebra of $\mathcal{O}_2$ generated by $X$. Since $X^2 = X + 1$, $\mathcal{A}$ is a 2-dimensional commutative subalgebra, isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}[T]/(T^2-T-1)$.
2. **Semisimplicity and Eigenspaces**:
   The polynomial $T^2 - T - 1$ has distinct roots $\phi, \psi$. Thus, $\mathcal{A}$ is semisimple and isomorphic to $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{C}$ as a ring. The identity decomposes into two orthogonal projections:
   $$ P_\phi = \frac{X - \psi}{\phi - \psi}, \quad P_\psi = \frac{X - \phi}{\psi - \phi} $$
   Since $Y$ commutes with $X$, it commutes with $P_\phi$ and $P_\psi$, meaning it preserves their corresponding eigenspaces.
3. **Roots of Unity in Eigenspaces**:
   Since $Y^m = X$ and $X$ acts as scalar multiplication by $\phi$ and $\psi$ on the respective eigenspaces, the restriction of $Y$ to the $\phi$-eigenspace must be an $m$-th root of $\phi$ (so $Y_\phi = \phi^{1/m} I_\phi$), and similarly $Y_\psi = \psi^{1/m} I_\psi$.
   Since $Y$ is a scalar on each eigenspace, it lies in the linear span of the projections $P_\phi, P_\psi$, which is exactly $\mathcal{A}$. Hence, $Y = a 1 + b X$ for some $a, b \in \mathbb{C}$.

## 4. Braid Flow Non-commutativity (`GoldenMeanShift.lean`)

### Target
`braid_flow_noncomm` in [GoldenMeanShift.lean](file:///home/goutev/repos/info-geometry-lean/lean/sandbox/GoldenMeanShift.lean#L120)

### Mathematical Statement
Conjugation of the Fibonacci braid representation generators $R, B$ by powers of $X$ preserves their non-commutativity:
$$ (X^k R X^{-k}) * (X^k B X^{-k}) \neq (X^k B X^{-k}) * (X^k R X^{-k}) $$

### Chain of Thought & Proof Architecture
1. **Automorphism Under Conjugation**:
   Since $X$ is invertible in $\mathcal{O}_2$ (with inverse $X - 1$), conjugation by $X$ defines an algebra automorphism:
   $$ \Phi_X(Y) = X Y X^{-1} $$
2. **Preservation of Algebraic Relations**:
   Any algebra automorphism preserves algebraic relations, including commutativity and non-commutativity:
   $$ Y_1 Y_2 \neq Y_2 Y_1 \iff \Phi(Y_1) \Phi(Y_2) \neq \Phi(Y_2) \Phi(Y_1) $$
3. **Injectivity of Representation**:
   Since the original Fibonacci braid representation generators $R, B$ do not commute ($R B \neq B R$), their image under the automorphism $\Phi_{X^k}$ must also not commute:
   $$ (X^k R X^{-k}) (X^k B X^{-k}) = X^k (R B) X^{-k} \neq X^k (B R) X^{-k} = (X^k B X^{-k}) (X^k R X^{-k}) $$

## 5. Subalgebra Isomorphism (`GoldenMeanShift.lean`)

### Target
`subalgebra_isomorphic_to_golden_quotient` in [GoldenMeanShift.lean](file:///home/goutev/repos/info-geometry-lean/lean/sandbox/GoldenMeanShift.lean#L131)

### Mathematical Statement
$$ \text{adjoin } \mathbb{C} \{X\} \cong \mathbb{C}[T]/(T^2-T-1) $$

### Chain of Thought & Proof Architecture
1. **Evaluation Homomorphism**:
   We define the algebra homomorphism $\text{eval}_X : \mathbb{C}[T] \to \mathcal{O}_2$ by evaluating a polynomial $Q(T)$ at $X$:
   $$ \text{eval}_X(Q) = Q(X) $$
   The image of $\text{eval}_X$ is by definition the subalgebra generated by $X$, which is $\text{adjoin } \mathbb{C} \{X\}$.
2. **Kernel Containment**:
   Since $X^2 - X - 1 = 0$, the polynomial $P(T) = T^2 - T - 1$ lies in the kernel of $\text{eval}_X$. Therefore, the kernel contains the ideal generated by $P(T)$:
   $$ (P(T)) \subseteq \ker(\text{eval}_X) $$
3. **Kernel Minimality (Non-scalarness)**:
   Since $\mathbb{C}[T]$ is a principal ideal domain (PID), $\ker(\text{eval}_X) = (Q(T))$ for some polynomial $Q(T)$ dividing $P(T)$.
   If $\ker(\text{eval}_X)$ were strictly larger than $(P(T))$, then $Q(T)$ would have to be of degree 1, meaning $X = \lambda 1$ for some root $\lambda \in \{\phi, \psi\}$.
   However, $X = \text{matrixToCuntz } 2 A$, and since $A$ is not a scalar matrix and the Cuntz representation is injective, $X$ is not a scalar. Thus, $Q(T)$ must have degree 2, meaning $\ker(\text{eval}_X) = (P(T))$.
   By the first isomorphism theorem, the quotient algebra is isomorphic to the image:
   $$ \mathbb{C}[T]/(T^2-T-1) \cong \text{adjoin } \mathbb{C} \{X\} $$
