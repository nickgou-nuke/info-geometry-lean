## 1. Your “fourth structure” has a precise existing interpretation

Start with three operators satisfying

\[
\sigma_i^2=1,
\qquad
\sigma_i\sigma_j=-\sigma_j\sigma_i
\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Their oriented product

\[
J:=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3
\]

satisfies

\[
J^2=-1,
\qquad
J\sigma_i=\sigma_iJ.
\]

Consequently,

\[
(J\sigma_i)^2=-1.
\]

This is the algebraic mechanism connecting hyperbolic and circular generators: a square-plus-one direction becomes a square-minus-one direction through multiplication by the **commuting volume element**.

## 4. Fourier, Mellin, Laplace, and Legendre have distinct roles

Your stream contains several kinds of duality. Keeping their types explicit makes the connections much stronger.

| Construction | What becomes dual | Principal mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Fourier | Translation coordinate and frequency | Characters of an additive group |
| Mellin | Positive scale and logarithmic frequency | Characters after taking logarithms |
| Laplace | A function and a complex spectral parameter | Exponential weighting; Fourier on vertical lines |
| Legendre | A convex potential and its dual potential | Optimization and the gradient map |
| Penrose | Twistor/cohomological data and spacetime fields | Incidence and contour/cohomology constructions |

The Fourier, Laplace, and Mellin distinctions agree with their standard integral definitions; convergence and inversion conditions are separate parts of the theory. [dlmf.nist.gov](https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.14?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 7. The clean connection between \(n\)-operator uncertainty and a doubled Dirac operator is a Gram matrix

This is the most useful common construction for the uncertainty portion of your question.

For a normalized state \(\psi\) and self-adjoint observables \(A_a\), define

\[
v_a=(A_a-\langle A_a\rangle)\psi,
\qquad
G_{ab}=\langle v_a,v_b\rangle.
\]

Then \(G\) is positive semidefinite because

\[
z^\dagger Gz
=
\left\|\sum_a z_a v_a\right\|^2
\ge0.
\]

Its real and imaginary parts are

\[
G=C+\frac i2\Omega,
\]

where

\[
C_{ab}
=
\frac12\langle\{\Delta A_a,\Delta A_b\}\rangle,
\qquad
\Omega_{ab}
=
-i\langle[A_a,A_b]\rangle.
\]

Thus the natural \(n\)-operator uncertainty statement is

\[
\boxed{C+\frac i2\Omega\succeq0.}
\]

The familiar two-operator bound is a principal-minor consequence:

\[
\operatorname{Var}(A)\operatorname{Var}(B)
\ge
C_{AB}^{\,2}
+\frac14|\langle[A,B]\rangle|^2.
\]

The repository already proves the corresponding bounded Hilbert-space QGT inequality and its Lie-representation specialization in [`QuantumGeometry/Projective/QGT.lean`](https://github.com/nickgou-nuke/info-geometry-lean/blob/08c34e03f610b452e03422037949196dc7d92d6d/lean/InfoGeometry/QuantumGeometry/Projective/QGT.lean):

- `QGT_eq_inner_projOrth`
- `QGT_cauchy_schwarz`
- `robertson_schrodinger_qgt_bound`
- `berryCurvature_eq_commutator`
- `lie_rep_uncertainty_bound`

\]

Its square is

\]

[`Krein/FiniteDiracGramSquare.lean`](https://github.com/nickgou-nuke/info-geometry-lean/blob/08c34e03f610b452e03422037949196dc7d92d6d/lean/InfoGeometry/Krein/FiniteDiracGramSquare.lean) proves this for arbitrary rectangular complex matrices, along with:

## 8. Fisher information, Cramér–Rao, and spacetime parameters fit through positive state geometry

\langle\psi,\partial_b\psi\rangle.

\[

### What “four-vector uncertainty” should mean here

There are two distinct constructions:

1. A positive covariance/commutator matrix for four observables.
2. A Fisher matrix for estimating four spacetime displacement parameters.

For example, a translation model generated by \(P_\mu\) can encode parameters through

\[
\psi(a)=e^{-ia^\mu P_\mu/\hbar}\psi_0.
\]

Its Fisher matrix measures distinguishability of displacements. The four translation generators commute; the estimation bound is not a claim of noncommutativity among the four momenta.

If an operator transformation law

\[
A'_a=\Lambda_a{}^b A_b
\]

is established, the Gram matrix transforms by

\[
G'=\Lambda G\Lambda^\mathsf T.
\]

Positivity survives this congruence. This gives a way to combine tensorial covariance with positive uncertainty bounds.

**The Minkowski metric itself is indefinite and cannot serve as a positive covariance matrix.** The repository’s [`Physics/FisherKreinLorentz.lean`](https://github.com/nickgou-nuke/info-geometry-lean/blob/08c34e03f610b452e03422037949196dc7d92d6d/lean/InfoGeometry/Physics/FisherKreinLorentz.lean) constructs a chosen sign reflection. Its later vielbein bridge retains explicit metric-calibration hypotheses. These results do not derive a Lorentzian metric from an arbitrary statistical Fisher matrix.

### The Fisher-to-Heisenberg bridge needs a phase term

One existing scalar bridge assumes

\[
\operatorname{Var}(P)=\frac14 I_F.
\]

For a sufficiently regular scalar wavepacket

\[
\psi=\sqrt{\rho}\,e^{i\phi},
\]

the more general calculation is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(P)
=
\frac{\hbar^2}{4}
\int\rho(\partial_x\log\rho)^2\,dx
+
\hbar^2\operatorname{Var}_\rho(\partial_x\phi).
\]

Equality with the Fisher term requires constant phase gradient, up to the relevant almost-everywhere qualification. Together with the location-score inequality and justified boundary terms, this yields the usual position–momentum uncertainty bound.

This is exactly where the existing Schwartz integration-by-parts theorems should replace a supplied momentum/Fisher identity. The current [`Canonical/CramerRaoUncertainty.lean`](https://github.com/nickgou-nuke/info-geometry-lean/blob/08c34e03f610b452e03422037949196dc7d92d6d/lean/InfoGeometry/Canonical/CramerRaoUncertainty.lean) proves the algebraic consequence of that supplied identity.
