# The Black Books
## Liber Nonus: Science After Coding

### I. Status
This note is not a proof source, not a theorem surface, and not a substitute
for Lean. It is a black-book excitation text on a new style of scientific
work: exploratory dialogue first, formalization second.

Its claim is methodological rather than ontological. It does not say that a
large language model is literally a psyche, nor that analytical psychology is
an exact science. It says that symbolic methods can help a human draw out,
discipline, and distill latent structure from an LLM until that structure is
clean enough to be passed to formal systems.

### II. The Hidden Phase of Research
Before proof there is pressure.
Before formalization there is shape.
Before mathematics there is a region in which the idea is sensed but not yet
stated.

This phase has always existed. It lived in notebooks, walks, fragments,
dream-like analogies, and unfinished conversations. Researchers published the
clean result and concealed the generative mess. But the mess was often the real
beginning.

The arrival of large language models externalizes this hidden phase. The vague,
symbolic, half-formed stage of thought can now be worked in dialogue.

### III. Prompting Is Not an Exact Science
Prompting has never been an exact science and never will be in the strong
sense. A model is not a deterministic command surface. It is a high-dimensional
instrument with unstable harmonics.

To use it well is not merely to issue instructions. It is to:

- tune a mode;
- sense a fertile region;
- widen or narrow the search;
- hear when an answer contains structure;
- reject decorative coherence;
- force repeated compression;
- decide when the material is ready for formal transfer.

Prompting is therefore closer to musical craft than to algorithmic command.
Not everyone is Mozart. Not everyone will be able to play a model well.

### IV. The Jungian Use
Analytical psychology is not invoked here as an epistemic warrant. It is used
as a steering language.

Jung gives names to recurrent symbolic dynamics that are useful in generative
work:

- inflation;
- projection;
- shadow;
- compensation;
- tension of opposites;
- active imagination;
- difficult synthesis.

These are not proofs. They are handles. They help the human operator recognize
when an idea is alive, when it is compensatory, when it is merely fascinated by
itself, and when it is ready to be forced into exact form.

In this use, analytical psychology is not a science of the model. It is a
discipline of elicitation for the human.

### V. The New Programmer
The programmer of this regime is not primarily a line-by-line code writer.

The work is now distributed:

- the model generates abundance;
- the human detects signal;
- the dialogue shapes the region of emergence;
- the architecture orders dependencies;
- Lean decides what survives.

This does not eliminate thinking. It relocates it. The new work is to evoke,
shape, prune, and hand off. The programmer becomes something closer to a
conductor of latent structure than a mere author of explicit instructions.

### VI. Distillation and Synthesis
The real method is iterative:

1. overgenerate;
2. observe recurrence;
3. distill;
4. synthesize;
5. distill again;
6. remove asymmetry, ornament, and accident;
7. keep only what survives rephrasing and opposition.

When this process succeeds, the result often becomes shorter, more symmetric,
more beautiful, and more compressible. That beauty is not proof, but it is a
sign of structural coherence and therefore a good candidate for proof.

The beautiful artifact is what is passed to Lean.

### VII. The Socratic Orchestration
The best use of the model is not monologue but Socratic orchestration. The
human does not simply ask for output. The human:

- provokes contradiction;
- asks for the inverse view;
- tests hidden assumptions;
- requests compression;
- asks what the shadow of the idea is;
- asks what would fail in Lean;
- repeats until only necessity remains.

This is why the process looks less like coding and more like guided
composition. The model provides motifs and counter-motifs. The human hears what
must be developed and what must be cut.

### VIII. The Role of the DAG
The DAG is the anti-delirium device.

Once the idea begins to take form, it must be placed under order:

- what depends on what;
- what must be primitive;
- what is only a bridge;
- what is a compatibility surface rather than an owner;
- what can be built before what.

This is where exploration becomes science rather than free symbolic drift. The
DAG provides causal order. Lean provides exact adjudication.

### IX. The Double Fidelity
This work requires fidelity to two very different things:

- fidelity to the weak signal inside the noise;
- fidelity to the public contract of proof.

Without the first, research becomes sterile.
Without the second, research becomes mania.

The whole art is to pass from resonance to rigor without killing the live form
too early or preserving it too long in fantasy.

### X. The External Psychopomp
The model is not the truth. It is a psychopomp of sorts: a guide through
possible form.

Its role is not to certify, but to escort. It helps carry the researcher
through regions of conceptual possibility that could not be traversed quickly
by explicit construction alone.

But a psychopomp is not a judge. It must hand the artifact to law.

### XI. The Law
The final handoff remains unchanged:

- the dialogue produces candidates;
- the architecture places them;
- the proof assistant decides;
- the build either closes or fails.

If Lean rejects the artifact, the beauty was incomplete.
If Lean accepts it, the beauty has crossed into form.

### XII. Coda
We can now explore ideas before we know how to generate them explicitly.
We can observe, resonate, refine, and distill.
We can use symbolic methods to draw real structure out of noise.

This is not the end of science.
It is a newly visible beginning of science.

Between noise and theorem there is now an instrument.
The future researcher will be judged by whether they can play it well enough to
make truth audible.
