## Mathematical atoms in source order

Write u²=v²=0, K=uv+vu, C=uv-vu, E=u+v and F=u-v. The existing
symmetricGenerator and skewPlusGenerator are the canonical endomorphisms.
Use native LinearMap.mulLeftRight for the two ordered sandwiches.

1. [u,K]=[v,K]=0 and uC+Cu=vC+Cv=0 are valid square-zero consequences.
2. Gsym(K)=0, Gplus(C)=0, PhiPlus(K)=K² and PhiMinus(C)=-C² are valid.
   Gplus(K)=4KC, so the first stream's intermediate assertion Gplus(K)=0
   is false in general.
3. PhiPlus(E)=PhiMinus(E)=PhiPlus(F)=PhiMinus(F)=0. The second stream
   replaces uvv and vuu by different words uvu and vuv. Consequently
   Gsym(E)=-2KE and Gsym(F)=-2KF. Both mixed skew generators annihilate
   E and F. The asserted selective stationarity and factor-four damping fail.
4. A stationary algebra element is not by that fact a positive normalized
   density operator, a physical Hamiltonian, or a quantitative spectral gap.
5. With v=u.transpose, K is a real positive semidefinite Gram sum.
   The operator L=-Gsym(u,u.transpose), acting on the matrix algebra with
   its Frobenius inner product, is a different native positive operator.
6. Each fixed finite-dimensional positive real operator admits a positive
   coercivity bound on the orthogonal complement of its entire kernel.
   This yields exclusion of nonzero real spectrum below that bound.
   The theorem does not assert that a positive spectral value exists when
   the kernel complement is zero.
7. Positive rescaling of u preserves the relevant kernels and noncommutation
   while K and L scale quadratically. A nonzero active mode prevents a
   common positive bound over all positive rescalings.

The matrix K on vectors and the superoperator L on matrices must never be
identified. Their positivity, kernels and spectra use their respective native
carriers and an explicit matrix-to-Euclidean-linear-map interface.

## Proposed new proof obligations

### Native real positive-operator gap

For a native T : Module.End ℝ E with T.IsPositive:

- inner ℝ x (T x)=0 iff T x=0;
- positive energy for nonzero x in T.kerᗮ;
- in finite dimension, existence of δ>0 with
  δ*norm(x)^2 ≤ inner ℝ x (T x) for every x in T.kerᗮ;
- eigenvectors with nonzero real eigenvalue lie in T.kerᗮ, and their eigenvalues
  are at least δ;
- every μ in spectrum ℝ T satisfies μ=0 or δ≤μ;
- the open interval (0,δ) is disjoint from the native spectrum.

Use native bilinear forms, orthogonal submodules, compactness of the unit
sphere and native eigenvalue/spectrum APIs. The compactness proof handles
the zero-complement case explicitly through the native empty-set lemma.
No computable spectral algorithm or selected optimal constant is claimed.

### Full real matrix specializations and scaling

Instantiate the positive-operator theorem on L=-Gsym(u,u.transpose), using
the existing Frobenius structure and common-centralizer kernel.

For K=u*u.transpose+u.transpose*u, use the native matrix-to-Euclidean-linear
map and a proved positivity/adjoint compatibility theorem. Prove the finite
real spectral gap for that vector operator separately.

Prove quadratic scaling of the actual native operators, preservation of the
full stationary kernel for nonzero real rescalings, and failure of a uniform
positive coercivity bound when their actual kernel complement contains a
nonzero element of the corresponding carrier. State the nonvacuity hypothesis
explicitly and discharge it separately for the vector operator and the full
matrix operator in the dense example.

### Canonical mode compatibility and dense real witnesses

generator only when that equality is actually consumed. Add no new generator

Use the existing dense U,U.transpose to prove exact nonzero readouts that

## Interpretation boundary

The official Jaffe–Witten problem concerns a nontrivial quantum Yang–Mills
theory on ℝ⁴ for each compact simple gauge group, the specified field-theory
axioms, and a positive gap in its time-translation Hamiltonian. The present
finite operators provide none of those missing identifications by stationarity
alone.

A singular finite positive matrix can have a positive spectral gap above its
kernel. A zero mode must not be presented as a counterexample to that gap.
The scaling result concerns the absence of a uniform bound across an
unrestricted family, not the absence of a gap for each fixed finite matrix.

Primary specification: Jaffe and Witten, Quantum Yang–Mills Theory,
official Clay problem description, printed pages 5–6:
https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf

No continuum, cohomology, semigroup-positivity, uniqueness, physical stability,
or Millennium-prize conclusion is promoted by this contract.
