## Three separate infinitesimal requirements

For a real linear functional `ω : A →ₗ[ℝ] ℝ`, a native bilinear form
`B : LinearMap.BilinForm ℝ A`, and `D : Module.End ℝ A`,
`IsOperatorialKillingField ω B D` is the conjunction

\[
\begin{aligned}
D(xy)&=D(x)y+xD(y),\\
\omega(Dx)&=0,\\
B(Dx,y)+B(x,Dy)&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first condition preserves the product infinitesimally. The second
annihilates the chosen functional. The third is the skew-adjointness equation
for the chosen form. The implementation keeps all three requirements; it
does not infer the latter two from the Leibniz law.

`operatorialKillingLieSubalgebra` is the native intersection

\[
\operatorname{Der}(A)\cap
\{D:\omega\circ D=0\}\cap\mathfrak{o}(B)
\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb R}(A).
\]

The bracket is actual composition commutator
`D ∘ E - E ∘ D`. The existing derivation owner supplies Leibniz closure;
`functionalAnnihilatingLieSubalgebra` supplies functional-annihilation closure;
Mathlib supplies the skew-adjoint Lie subalgebra. Thus
`lieSpan_le_operatorialKilling` bounds every finite Lie expression and real
linear combination generated by an admissible set. Its `_iff` version
identifies this bound exactly with the primitive conditions on the generators.
This is algebraic Lie generation, not topological closure or a statement about
the Lie algebra of a topological automorphism group.

A functional here is not automatically a positive normalized state. A form
here is not automatically symmetric, nondegenerate, or positive. These
properties are unnecessary for the generic closure theorem and must be
established separately when an interpretation requires them.

## Bounded analytic integration on the same real algebra

The integration and final bridge require

```lean
[NormedRing A] [NormedAlgebra ℝ A] [CompleteSpace A]
D : A →L[ℝ] A
ω : A →L[ℝ] ℝ
B : A →L[ℝ] A →L[ℝ] ℝ
```

Here `D` is an everywhere-defined bounded continuous linear operator.
The functional and the bilinear form are continuous. `continuousBilinForm`
forgets the latter continuity proofs without changing either argument or
any value; `D.toLinearMap` and `ω.toLinearMap` do the corresponding algebraic
forgetting. The analytic and algebraic statements therefore concern the
same carrier and operations.

The proof chain is explicit:

1. The existing owner defines `flow D t = NormedSpace.exp (t • D)` in the
   Banach algebra of continuous endomorphisms. It supplies the time-addition
   law, inverse `flow (-D) t`, and the orbit equation
   `d/dt (flow D t x) = D (flow D t x)`.
2. The Leibniz identity gives the differential equation for the product of
   two orbits. The existing interaction-picture argument differentiates
   `flow (-D) t (flow D t x * flow D t y)`, obtains zero, and applies the
   real mean-value theorem to establish multiplicativity.
3. `derivation_map_one` derives `D 1 = 0` from Leibniz. The existing fixed-point
   theorem then gives unit preservation. Together with the actual inverse
   linear equivalence, this constructs `flowAlgEquiv`, followed by
   `flowAlgEquivHom : Multiplicative ℝ →* (A ≃ₐ[ℝ] A)`.
4. The chain rule makes the derivative of `ω (flow D t x)` zero when
   `ω ∘ D = 0`. The continuous bilinear product rule makes the derivative
   of `B (flow D t x) (flow D t y)` zero under the skewness equation.
   The real mean-value theorem yields both conservation statements.
5. `boundedKillingFlowHom` consumes the three infinitesimal conditions.
   `hasDerivAt_boundedKillingFlow_zero` recovers the original generator,
   and `boundedKillingFlow_mem_stabilizer` derives finite-time invariance
   from those conditions.
6. Conversely, an actual automorphism homomorphism with specified orbit
   derivatives at zero yields the Leibniz equation by differentiating
   product preservation. Differentiating conserved continuous functionals
   and bilinear forms yields annihilation and skewness. This reverse
   implication needs no completeness; its orbit derivatives are explicit
   hypotheses. `isOperatorialKilling_iff_exists_invariant_flow` combines the
   two directions in the complete setting: the three Killing equations are
   equivalent to existence of a preserving algebra-automorphism flow whose
   derivative at zero is the specified bounded continuous generator.

Only scalar multiples of a single generator are used in its exponential
addition law. Different generators in a family need not commute. No identity
equating a product of their exponentials with the exponential of their sum
is added.

These declarations do not integrate an arbitrary unbounded derivation or
construct a Lie-group manifold structure on the automorphism group.

## Group actions and the carrier-dependent closure

Let `G = A ≃ₐ[ℝ] A`. With
`open scoped OperatorInvariantPullback`, the selected left actions are

\[
(\alpha\cdot\omega)(x)=\omega(\alpha^{-1}x),\qquad
(\alpha\cdot B)(x,y)=B(\alpha^{-1}x,\alpha^{-1}y).
\]

The scope registers both the scalar-action projections and their group laws;
the form action transforms both arguments. Importing the owner alone does
not globally replace other actions on linear-map types.

`functional_fixed_iff` and `bilinForm_fixed_iff` convert fixed inverse
pullback to the forward equations `ω (α x) = ω x` and
`B (α x) (α y) = B x y`. The common subgroup is exactly

\[
K_{\omega,B}=\operatorname{Stab}_G(\omega)
\cap\operatorname{Stab}_G(B)=\operatorname{Stab}_G(\omega,B).
\]

Choose the invariant carrier

\[
C=(A\to_{\mathbb R}\mathbb R)\times
\operatorname{Bilin}_{\mathbb R}(A),\qquad
\operatorname{cl}_C(H)=\operatorname{Fix}_G(C^H).
\]

The ambient `G` consists of algebraic real algebra equivalences, without a
bundled continuity or star-preservation requirement. The invariant carrier
contains all algebraic functionals and forms. The analytic flow maps are
constructed from bounded operators, but this does not change the ambient
group or silently replace `C` with a carrier of continuous invariants.

For supplied preserving automorphism families, or for the bounded Killing
families constructed analytically above, the final chain is

\[
H_\Phi\leq\operatorname{cl}_C(H_\Phi)\leq K_{\omega,B}.
\]

`H_Φ` uses native `Subgroup.closure` to generate a subgroup from all time
maps. `cl_C` is the separate native Galois closure. One invariant pair gives
containment, not equality with its full stabilizer. The full stabilizer
itself is closed, and `symmetryClosure_le_functionalForm_iff` states that a
subgroup and its closure have exactly the same common-stabilizer bound.

The generic group theorem permits an arbitrary time monoid and assumes its
displayed preservation equations. The bounded analytic bridge specializes
to all real times and proves those equations from the generators. Neither
construction identifies closures computed on different invariant carriers.

## Full real matrix specialization and counterexample

`RealMatrixKillingErlangen` works on **all** `Matrix n n ℝ`, for finite `n`
with decidable equality. Its functional is the ordinary matrix trace, and
its native bilinear form is the existing pairing

\[
\omega(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X),\qquad B(X,Y)=\operatorname{Tr}(XY).
\]

The owner connects `innerDerivative H X = HX-XH` to all three infinitesimal
conditions. It constructs the monoid homomorphism from matrix units to
algebra automorphisms, `X ↦ uXu⁻¹`, and derives preservation of trace and
the form for every invertible matrix. Unit-valued motions then supply
actual automorphism motions to the generic family closure theorem.
This does not identify an arbitrary supplied unit-valued motion with an
exponential of a particular inner derivation.

The trace pairing is symmetric and nondegenerate. It is explicitly
indefinite on two-by-two real matrices:

\[
B(I,I)=2,\qquad B(J,J)=-2,\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus arbitrary invertible conjugation invariance is not being presented as
orthogonal invariance for a positive Hilbert--Schmidt form.

For the actual noncommuting matrix units

\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
L=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\ell(X)=X_{00},
\]

the implementation evaluates `ℓ([R,L]) = 1`. Hence `ad_R` satisfies the
genuine Leibniz rule but does not annihilate the independently chosen
functional `ℓ`. This is the concrete content of
`leibniz_not_imply_functional_invariance`; it refutes the intake's implication
from Leibniz alone to the full Killing conditions.

A second counterexample keeps the trace functional fixed. For
`H = diag(1, 0)` and the positive Frobenius pairing
`F(X,Y) = Tr(XᵀY)`, the inner derivation `ad_H` satisfies Leibniz and
annihilates trace, but

\[
F([H,R],R)+F(R,[H,R])=2.
\]

`frobenius_killing_defect` proves this value and
`leibniz_trace_not_imply_frobenius_killing` exposes the failure of universal
skewness. Functional invariance therefore does not make the bilinear-form
condition redundant.

## Boundaries and literature

- **Complete positivity:** the defect
  `Γ(x,y) = D(xy) - D(x)y - xD(y)` vanishes exactly when the Leibniz equation
  holds. Its nonvanishing does not establish a Lindblad generator, positivity,
  complete positivity, contractivity, or restriction to forward time. A
  bounded linear exponential has negative-time inverses as linear maps even
  when it fails to preserve multiplication. Lindblad's
  [1976 paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01608499) characterizes
  bounded generators of completely positive quantum dynamical semigroups
  under additional operator-algebraic conditions.
- **Bounded versus general operator dynamics:** Takesaki's
  [lecture notes, Theorem 4.9](https://www.imsc.res.in/~sunder/mtnotes.pdf)
  discuss bounded exponential automorphism groups for everywhere-defined
  derivations of complex C*-algebras and distinguish algebra automorphisms
  from star automorphisms. That literature result is not imported as an
  axiom, automatic boundedness theorem, or realification theorem here.
- **Modular theory:** no faithful normal state, cyclic separating vector,
  Tomita operator, modular operator, or unbounded modular generator is
  constructed by these owners. The finite trace example is not a Type-III
  state model. Witten's [notes, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.04993v6)
  distinguish the finite-density conjugation formula from its general
  modular implementation; an identification with the present real flow
  requires a separate carrier and intertwining theorem.
- **Koopman/Arnold:** a classical specialization requires an actual function
  algebra, an invariant integration functional with its domain and continuity
  hypotheses, and compatibility of pullback with the chosen actions.
  Inverse pullback along a positive-time geometric flow has generator
  `-L_X`, so the sign must be matched. The generic closure pattern alone
  does not prove this construction or establish a quantization duality.
