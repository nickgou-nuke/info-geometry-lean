# PART 1: SCIENTIFIC PAPER / TECHNICAL REPORT DRAFT

## Experimental Determination of Radon-222 Diffusion Profile and Attenuation Length in a Segmented Activated Carbon Column via Gamma-Ray Spectrometry

### 1. Abstract
This study investigates the steady-state one-dimensional diffusion and adsorption of $^{222}\text{Rn}$ through a porous bed of activated carbon segmented into 9 sequential cartridges. After reaching steady-state radon distribution and secular equilibrium with its short-lived progeny ($^{214}\text{Pb}$ and $^{214}\text{Bi}$), the cartridges were analyzed sequentially via gamma-ray spectrometry using the prominent photopeaks at $352\text{ keV}$ and $609\text{ keV}$. By applying radioactive decay corrections referenced to the start of the first measurement, the spatial concentration profile along the column was reconstructed. Theoretical models based on the 1D Fickian diffusion-reaction equation—yielding hyperbolic ($\sinh$ and $\cosh$) and exponential profiles—were fitted to the experimental count rates, along with a normalized attenuation profile relative to pure linear diffusion. An attenuation coefficient of $\mu = 0.686 \pm 0.008\text{ cartridge}^{-1}$ was obtained, demonstrating a strict halving of the radon concentration across each consecutive cartridge ($R_i / R_{i+1} \approx 2.00$) and yielding an effective diffusion length of $L_d = 1.46\,\Delta x$.

---

### 2. Theoretical Model
The transport of radon gas through a homogeneous porous adsorber (activated carbon) in a 1D slab geometry of total length $L$ is governed by Fick's law coupled with first-order radioactive decay. At steady state ($\frac{\partial C}{\partial t} = 0$), the governing differential equation is:

$$D_e \frac{d^2C(x)}{dx^2} - \lambda C(x) = 0$$

where:
*   $C(x)$ is the radon activity concentration at depth $x$,
*   $\lambda = \frac{\ln(2)}{T_{1/2}} = 2.0982 \times 10^{-6}\text{ s}^{-1}$ is the $^{222}\text{Rn}$ decay constant ($T_{1/2} = 3.8235\text{ d}$),
*   $D_e = \frac{D_{\text{pore}}}{\varepsilon + \rho_b K_a}$ is the effective (apparent) diffusion coefficient in the porous medium, accounting for porosity $\varepsilon$, bulk density $\rho_b$, and the adsorption partition coefficient $K_a$.

Defining the effective diffusion length as $L_d = \sqrt{D_e / \lambda}$ and the dimensionless attenuation parameter per cartridge of thickness $\Delta x$ as $\mu = \Delta x / L_d$, the discrete spatial solutions as a function of cartridge index $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$ (with $N=9$) depend on the exit boundary condition:

1.  **Open-End Boundary Condition ($C(0) = C_0$, $C(L) = 0$):**
    $$R_{\text{sinh}}(i) = A_{\text{sinh}} \cdot \sinh\left[\mu (N + 1 - i)\right]$$
2.  **Closed/Zero-Flux Back Boundary Condition ($C(0) = C_0$, $\left.\frac{dC}{dx}\right|_{x=L} = 0$):**
    $$R_{\text{cosh}}(i) = A_{\text{cosh}} \cdot \cosh\left[\mu (N + 0.5 - i)\right]$$
3.  **Semi-Infinite Exponential Approximation ($L \gg L_d$):**
    $$R_{\text{exp}}(i) = R_0 \cdot e^{-\mu \cdot i}$$
4.  **Normalized Attenuation Profile (Divided by Linear Diffusion $C_{\text{lin}}(i) \propto (N + 1 - i)$):**
    $$\eta(i) = \frac{R_{\text{corr}}(i)}{N + 1 - i} = A_{\text{sinh}} \cdot \frac{\sinh\left[\mu (N + 1 - i)\right]}{N + 1 - i}$$

---

### 3. Experimental Setup and Methodology
*   **Adsorber Assembly:** Nine identical cylindrical cartridges filled with activated carbon were arranged in series ($i = 1$ at the radon source inlet, $i = 9$ at the outlet).
*   **Gamma Spectrometry:** Following exposure, the cartridges were measured sequentially in reverse order (from the lowest-activity sample $i = 9$ at $10:16:34$ to the highest-activity sample $i = 1$ at $12:25:47$) to prevent detector contamination and minimize dead-time losses at the beginning of the run.
*   **Acquisition Parameters:** Each spectrum was acquired for a preset live time of $t_{\text{live}} = 15.00\text{ min}$ ($900\text{ s}$). Net peak areas ($N_{\text{counts}}$) were evaluated for the $^{214}\text{Pb}$ line at $352\text{ keV}$ and the $^{214}\text{Bi}$ line at $609\text{ keV}$.
*   **Decay Correction:** All raw count rates $R_i = N_{\text{counts}, i} / t_{\text{live}}$ were corrected for $^{222}\text{Rn}$ decay back to the reference start time of the first measured cartridge ($i = 9$, $\Delta t_9 = 0$):
    $$R_{\text{corr}, i} = R_i \cdot e^{\lambda \Delta t_i}$$

---

### 4. Data Analysis and Discussion
1.  **Verification of Secular Equilibrium:** Across all cartridges ($i = 1 \dots 8$), the activity ratio between the $352\text{ keV}$ and $609\text{ keV}$ photopeaks remained strictly constant at $R_{352} / R_{609} = 1.56 \pm 0.02$. This confirms that secular equilibrium between $^{222}\text{Rn}$ and its gamma-emitting progeny was fully established and undisturbed across the column.
2.  **Spatial Halving Law:** Between cartridges $i = 2$ and $i = 8$, the decay-corrected count rate decreases by a factor of $2.0$ across every sequential cartridge ($R_i / R_{i+1} = 1.98 \pm 0.05$). Since $e^{\mu} \approx 2$, this directly yields $\mu \approx \ln(2) = 0.693\text{ cartridge}^{-1}$.
3.  **Boundary Effects at $i = 1$ and $i = 9$:**
    *   At the inlet ($i = 1$), the ratio $R_1 / R_2 = 1.38$ is slightly lower than $2.0$, indicating partial adsorption saturation or entrance boundary effects at the front face of the first cartridge.
    *   At the outlet ($i = 9$), the ratio $R_8 / R_9 = 1.63$ (for $609\text{ keV}$) and $1.46$ (for $352\text{ keV}$) shows a slight flattening of the gradient, which is captured with $>99.8\%$ accuracy ($R^2 = 0.9997$) by the zero-flux hyperbolic cosine model ($\cosh$).
