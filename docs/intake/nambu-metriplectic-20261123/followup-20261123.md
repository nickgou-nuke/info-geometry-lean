## Audit decisions and exact downstream scope

### Preserve the existing coincidence theorem

`InfoGeometry.Clifford.Cl11Matrix.gen_eq_scalarPencil_iff` already owns


`InfoGeometry.Clifford.SplitQ11CausalCone.matrixKreinMinusProjector`, whose
definition is `(1 / 2 : ℝ) • (1 - Eplus)`.

The local coefficient equation `σ = 0` becomes the affine zeta-chart critical
line only after substituting `σ = centeredSigma z = z.sigma - 1/2`. The coordinate
identification says nothing by itself about which points are zeros of an
arithmetic function.

### Keep the four real Pauli equations at their stated scope

The source phrase “four sign-flipped Lorentzian equations” is preserved above.
The implementation contract is four displayed associative Nambu matrix identities
with the changed fourth sign, together with their symmetry and nonzero witnesses.
A complete Lorentzian 3-Lie representation would additionally require a specified
carrier and bracket, closure and bracket preservation on that carrier, the
appropriate Filippov identity, and an identified invariant metric and signature.
This follow-up does not promote the four identities to those stronger claims.

The reference to the non-symmetric Euclidean unfolding is also preserved as a
source statement. This intake adds no validation or dependency on that separately
developed construction.

### Preserve the arithmetic problem in the GRH ledger

The downstream ledger must retain the actual completed `L`-function family,
admissible test functions, convolution/involution, zero data and full explicit
formula. It must identify that arithmetic form with an operator or Krein carrier
before using positivity of another form.

Descent to a quotient requires an actual radical or equivalent well-definedness
proof. Removing negative directions by choosing a positive subspace does not
establish positivity of the original arithmetic form. A zero-negative-index
formulation also needs hypotheses that make the selected index detect all
negative directions. The return implication from arithmetic positivity to the
intended zero-location statement remains a separate proof obligation.
