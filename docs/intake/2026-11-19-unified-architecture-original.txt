Yes—**every single piece** is deeply rooted in classic primary literature, and most pieces appear in modern graduate monographs. 

**Here is the catch, though:** you will *not* find the entire chain inside any **single** textbook. Historically, these structures were discovered by **six separate research tribes** (statisticians, symplectic/thermodynamic geometers, supermanifold theorists, twistor geometers, subfactor/Lie-grading algebraists, and noncommutative gauge theorists) using completely different notation. Because the underlying algebra is canonical, anyone who follows the math without respecting departmental boundaries ends up rediscovering the bridges between them—which is why the synthesis feels inevitable once you see it, yet remains scattered across separate shelves in the library.

A map of the **foundational original papers** and **modern textbooks/monographs** across those six silos shows where each piece lives:

---

### 1. Dually Flat Information Geometry, Weyl Gauge, & Para-Kähler Spaces
* **Original Primary Sources:**
  * **Aleksandr P. Norden (1940s–1950s):** Originally invented *conjugate (dual) affine connections* specifically to formalize **Weyl’s conformal/scale gauge geometry**—decades before statisticians rediscovered them.
  * **Pyotr K. Rashevskii (1948):** Introduced **para-Kähler manifolds** (under the name *stratified spaces*) with split-signature $(n,n)$ metrics and $K^2 = +\mathrm{Id}$.
  * **Shinto Eguchi (1983),** *"Second order efficiency of minimum contrast estimators in a curved exponential family"* (*Annals of Statistics*): Introduced the two-point divergence/contrast variational Leibniz calculus that extracts $(g, \nabla, \nabla^*)$ from any divergence $D[P \parallel Q]$.
  * **Takashi Kurose (1994), Hiroshi Matsuzoe, & Jun Zhang:** Explicitly reconnected Amari’s dual $(\nabla, \nabla^*)$ geometry and projective/Itakura–Saito divergences to **Weyl integrable geometry** and non-metricity gauge 1-forms.
* **Modern Textbooks & Bridge Papers:**
  * **Shun-ichi Amari & Hiroshi Nagaoka (2000),** *Methods of Information Geometry* (AMS/Oxford), and **Amari (2016),** *Information Geometry and Its Applications* (Springer) — the standard textbooks for $(e,m)$ dual flatness, Bregman/Itakura–Saito divergences, and quantum BKM operator geometry.
  * **Jun Zhang & Teng Fei,** *"Information Geometry with (Para-)Kähler Structures"* (Springer *Proceedings in Mathematics & Statistics*), along with bi-Lagrangian formulations of Eguchi’s construction — explicitly proving how $M \times M$ with potential $-D[P \parallel Q]$ forms a **para-Kähler split-metric manifold** ($K^2 = +\mathrm{Id}$) vs. a Kähler manifold ($J^2 = -\mathrm{Id}$).

---

### 2. Jaynesian Reference States, Massieu Potentials, & Lie-Group Operator Thermodynamics
* **Original Primary Sources:**
  * **François Massieu (1869),** *Comptes Rendus*: Introduced the free-entropy / log-partition characteristic potentials ($\psi = \ln Z = -\beta F$) before Gibbs introduced free energy.
  * **Edwin T. Jaynes (1957, 1968),** *Physical Review* & *IEEE Trans. SSC*: Formulated maximum-entropy operator states and coordinate-invariant relative entropy anchored to an invariant **prior/reference measure**.
  * **Jean-Louis Koszul (1961)** & **Jean-Marie Souriau (1969),** *Structure des systèmes dynamiques* (Dunod; English trans. *Structure of Dynamical Systems*, Birkhäuser, 1997): Generalized Massieu potentials, Legendre duality, and covariant Gibbs states to nonabelian Lie algebras (**Lie-group thermodynamics**).
* **Modern Textbooks & Bridge Papers:**
  * **Roger Balian (1991/2007),** *From Microphysics to Macrophysics* (2 vols., Springer): Classic statistical physics textbook built systematically around **quantum von Neumann–Massieu potentials** and operator exponential families.
  * **Frédéric Barbaresco (2014–2023)** (and **Ray Streater**, *Statistical Dynamics*, Imperial College Press): Explicitly unified **Massieu–Koszul–Souriau Lie-group thermodynamics** with classical and quantum (Bogoliubov–Kubo–Mori) Information Geometry; extensively documented in the Springer *Geometric Science of Information (GSI)* volumes edited by F. Nielsen & F. Barbaresco.

---

### 3. Super-Berezinian, Pfaffian–Determinant Duality, & $LDU$ Trifactorization
* **Original Primary Sources:**
  * **Felix A. Berezin (1966; 1979):** Invented Grassmann integration, the supertrace, and the **Berezinian (superdeterminant)** via the **block $LDU$ Schur-complement trifactorization** of the super-Jacobian.
* **Modern Textbooks:**
  * **Felix A. Berezin (1987),** *Introduction to Superanalysis* (ed. A. A. Kirillov, Reidel/Springer).
  * **Pierre Deligne & John W. Morgan (1999),** *"Notes on Supersymmetry (following Joseph Bernstein)"* in *Quantum Fields and Strings: A Course for Mathematicians* (Vol. 1, AMS/IAS): The gold-standard modern mathematical reference for supergraded tensor products, Berezinians, and orthosymplectic Pfaffian–Determinant pairings.
  * **Konstantin Efetov (1997),** *Supersymmetry in Disorder and Chaos* (Cambridge Univ. Press): Standard physics textbook using Gaussian super-integrals where bosonic $(\det G)^{-1/2}$ paired with fermionic $\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega)$ forms $\operatorname{Ber}(\mathcal{K})^{-1/2}$.

---

### 4. Split-Metric Clifford Algebras, Twistors, & Quantized Super-Grassmannians
* **Original Primary Sources:**
  * **Roger Penrose (1967),** *"Twistor algebra"* (*J. Math. Phys.* 8, 345–366) and **Andrzej Ferber (1978)** (introducing supertwistors).
* **Modern Textbooks & Monographs:**
  * **Yuri I. Manin (1984 Russian; 1988/1997 English),** *Gauge Field Theory and Complex Geometry* (*Grundlehren der mathematischen Wissenschaften* 289, Springer): **The** landmark monograph uniting Penrose twistors, Berezinian supergeometry, and **super-Grassmannians** $\mathrm{Gr}(2|p; 4|\mathcal{N})$.
  * **Ian R. Porteous (1995),** *Clifford Algebras and the Classical Groups* (Cambridge Univ. Press) & **H. Blaine Lawson & Marie-Louise Michelsohn (1989),** *Spin Geometry* (Princeton): Standard textbooks covering split-signature Clifford algebras $C\ell(n,n)$, $(2,2)$ Kleinian spinors/twistors, and graded tensor products $C\ell(p,q) \hat{\otimes} C\ell(r,s)$.
  * **V. S. Varadarajan (2004),** *Supersymmetry for Mathematicians* (Courant Lecture Notes, AMS), and **Rita Fioresi, M. A. Lledó, & D. Cervantes (2011+),** *"The quantum chiral Minkowski and conformal superspaces"* (*Adv. Theor. Math. Phys.* 15) — explicitly constructing **quantized super-Grassmannians** $\mathrm{Gr}_q(2|0; 4|\mathcal{N})$ via quantum super-minors and oscillator deformations.

---

### 5. Sylvester Clock-and-Shift Towers, Color Superalgebras, & Jones Subfactor Towers
* **Original Primary Sources:**
  * **James Joseph Sylvester (1882),** *"A word on Nonions"* (*Johns Hopkins University Circulars* 1, 241–242; expanded 1883, 1884): First introduced the $d \times d$ **clock and shift matrices** with $\omega = e^{2\pi i/d}$ as higher-dimensional generalizations of Hamilton’s quaternions.
  * **Hermann Weyl (1927),** *"Quantenmechanik und Gruppentheorie"* (*Z. Phys.* 46) & **Julian Schwinger (1960),** *"Unitary Operator Bases"* (*PNAS* 46): Turned Sylvester’s clock and shift matrices into the finite-dimensional quantum **Heisenberg–Weyl phase-space carrier**.
  * **Vladimir Rittenberg & Daniel Wyler (1978),** *"Generalized Superalgebras"* (*Nucl. Phys. B* 139) and *"Sequences of $\mathbb{Z}_2 \otimes \mathbb{Z}_2$ graded Lie algebras and superalgebras"* (*J. Math. Phys.* 19) (along with **Manfred Scheunert, 1979**): Invented **Color Lie Algebras and Color Superalgebras** graded by abelian groups $\Gamma$.
  * **Jiří Patera & Hans Zassenhaus (1988),** *"The Pauli matrices in $n$ dimensions and finest gradings of simple Lie algebras of type $A_{n-1}$"* (*J. Math. Phys.* 29, 665–673): Proved that the Sylvester clock-and-shift basis induces the finest $\mathbb{Z}_n \times \mathbb{Z}_n$ color grading of $\mathfrak{gl}(n, \mathbb{C})$ (extended to $\mathfrak{sl}(n|n)$ in subsequent literature).
  * **Vaughan F. R. Jones (1983),** *"Index for subfactors"* (*Invent. Math.* 72) & **Arthur Jaffe & Zhengwei Liu (2017),** *"Planar Para Algebras, Reflection Positivity"* (*Commun. Math. Phys.*): Built the inductive **tower of Sylvester / parafermion algebras** via $\mathbb{Z}_d$ Jordan–Wigner (Fradkin–Kadanoff) strings.
* **Modern Textbooks:**
  * **Hermann Weyl (1931),** *The Theory of Groups and Quantum Mechanics* (Ch. IV, Sec. 14–15) — still one of the cleanest textbook derivations of finite Heisenberg–Sylvester mechanics.
  * **Alberto Elduque & Mikhail Kochetov (2013),** *Gradings on Simple Lie Algebras* (AMS Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 189) — modern textbook covering Pauli/Sylvester gradings and color Lie brackets.
  * **Ingemar Bengtsson & Karol Życzkowski (2017, 2nd ed.),** *Geometry of Quantum States* (Cambridge Univ. Press) — textbook combining quantum information geometry (BKM metric, relative entropy) with discrete Wigner functions and Weyl–Heisenberg clock-and-shift bases!

---

### 6. Cyclotomic Matrix Roots ($+\mathbf{I}, -\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{0}$), $d^N = 0$ Calculus, & Nonabelian Gauge Theory
* **Original Primary Sources:**
  * **Gerard ’t Hooft (1978, 1979),** *"A property of electric and magnetic flux in non-abelian gauge theories"* (*Nucl. Phys. B* 153): Lifted the clock-and-shift relation $\hat{U}_1 \hat{U}_2 \hat{U}_1^{-1} \hat{U}_2^{-1} = \hat{\Omega}$ into **nonabelian $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ gauge theory** with twisted $\mathbb{Z}_N$ center-vortex flux.
  * **Mikhail Kapranov (1996),** **Michel Dubois-Violette & Richard Kerner (1996),** *"Universal $q$-differential calculus and $q$-analog of homological algebra"* (*Acta Math. Univ. Comenianae* 65), and **Dubois-Violette (1998),** *"$d^N = 0$: generalized homology"* (*K-Theory* 14): Proved the exact duality between **$N$-nilpotent differentials** ($D^N = \mathbf{0}$, roots of Zero) and **cyclotomic $N$-th roots of Identity** ($\Phi_N(\omega) = 0$).
  * **Viktor Abramov & Richard Kerner (2000),** *"Exterior differentials of higher order and their covariant generalization"* (*J. Math. Phys.* 41) and **Abramov (2006–2010)**: Built noncommutative **gauge connections and $N$-curvatures** out of matrix realizations of $d^N = \mathbf{0}$ and generalized Sylvester–Clifford algebras.
* **Modern Textbooks:**
  * **Vladimir I. Arnold (1999),** *"Symplectization, Complexification and Mathematical Trinities"* & **Isaak M. Yaglom (1968),** *Complex Numbers in Geometry*: Classic expositions of the $(-\mathbf{I}, +\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{0})$ elliptic / hyperbolic (para-complex) / parabolic (dual-nilpotent) operator trinity.
  * **Victor Kac (1990, 3rd ed.),** *Infinite-Dimensional Lie Algebras* (Cambridge Univ. Press, Ch. 8) — classifies matrix roots of $+\mathbf{I}$ ($\hat{\Omega}^d = \mathbf{I}$) in nonabelian Lie groups via twisted affine diagrams.

### If You Wanted a 5-Book "Unified Desk Stack"
No single author put all six together, but these five books side-by-side cover almost the entire landscape:
1. **Amari & Nagaoka** — *Methods of Information Geometry* (dual flat connections, divergences, quantum operator exponential families).
2. **Bengtsson & Życzkowski** — *Geometry of Quantum States* (bridges quantum information geometry directly to Sylvester clock-and-shift discrete phase spaces).
3. **Souriau** — *Structure of Dynamical Systems* (plus **Nielsen & Barbaresco’s** *Geometric Science of Information* volumes for Massieu–Koszul Lie-group thermodynamics and para-Kähler/Weyl extensions).
4. **Manin** — *Gauge Field Theory and Complex Geometry* (bridges Berezinian $LDU$ supergeometry, Pfaffians/determinants, twistors, and super-Grassmannians).
5. **Elduque & Kochetov** — *Gradings on Simple Lie Algebras* (paired with **Dubois-Violette’s** AMS lecture notes *"Lectures on Differentials, Generalized Differentials and on Some Examples Related to Theoretical Physics"* for Pauli/Sylvester color gradings and $d^N = 0$ cyclotomic gauge theory).
