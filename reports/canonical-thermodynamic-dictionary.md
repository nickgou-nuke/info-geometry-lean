## Naming discipline

- `chiralAnomaly` is the operator-valued obstruction. It is not the chemical
  potential.
- The scalar quantity extracted from the anomaly, such as `chiralScale`, is the
  thermodynamic source term. When read thermodynamically, that is the right
  place for a chemical-potential interpretation.
- `PerelmanW.WFunctional` is a flow-level monotone response functional. It is
  not a primitive partition or log-potential.
- `GeneratedFlow` and `GeometricResponse` are intentionally high-level names.
  Their physical aliases should be used in prose, not in the kernel itself.

## Recommended prose

- Use "relative statistical weight" for raw multiplicative data.
- Use "Massieu potential" for `log Z`.
- Use "free energy" or "effective free-energy potential" for `-(1 / β) log Z`.
- Use "anomaly chemical potential" for scalar anomaly sources such as
  `chiralScale`, not for `chiralAnomaly`.
- Use "effective Planck constant" for `unitOfAction` when emphasizing
  noncommutativity scale.
- Use "modular transport" for the flow generated by the modular Hamiltonian.
- Use "information stress response" for downstream Hessian, Fisher, curvature,
  or anomaly response quantities.

## Current architectural reading

The preferred thermodynamic reading of the repo is:

`relative weight -> logarithmic generator -> generated flow -> geometric response`

with coarse graining and partition hierarchy inserted between relative weight
and generated flow:

`relative weight -> fiber partition -> effective potential -> generated flow -> geometric response`

Under this reading:

- grand-canonical thermodynamics gives the local partition calculus,
- information bottleneck gives the coarse-graining variational mechanism,
- cocycle and modular files give the logarithmic transport layer,
- `PerelmanW` reads the monotone response of the induced flow.
