The important point is that it is not one manifold. It is a **tower of Klein models** sharing the same split (D_4) parent.

The stable parent is

[
Cl(1,1)^{\widehat\otimes 4}
\cong
Cl(4,4)
\cong
M_{16}(\mathbb R).
]

Here the vector carrier is (\mathbb R^{4,4}), which is 8-dimensional. The full real spinor/Fock module has dimension (16), while the two chiral spinor modules have dimension (8) each. So (PGL(8,\mathbb R)) is the natural projective group for the vector and chiral-spinor triality siblings, while (PGL(16,\mathbb R)) acts on the full spinor/Fock module. The full Clifford algebra (M_{16}(\mathbb R)) has dimension (256) as a real vector space, so one should not confuse the algebra, the vector module, and the spinor module. The real Clifford classification gives (Cl(4,4)\cong M_{16}(\mathbb R)), and it also records the important asymmetry (Cl(3,1)\cong M_4(\mathbb R)) versus (Cl(1,3)\cong M_2(\mathbb H)). ([Wikipedia][1])

The (SO(4,4)) / (Spin(4,4)) layer is special because it is split (D_4). In this signature, the vector representation (V_8) and the two chiral spinor representations (S_+), (S_-) are all real 8-dimensional triality siblings. That is precisely the algebraic mechanism you want: spacetime vectors, left spinors, and right spinors are not forced into a hierarchy; they are three projective faces of the same split parent. ([arXiv][2])

So the representation skeleton is:

[
V_8
;\longleftrightarrow;
S_+
;\longleftrightarrow;
S_-.
]

Projectivizing gives the three sibling geometries:

[
\mathbb P(V_8),
\qquad
\mathbb P(S_+),
\qquad
\mathbb P(S_-).
]

That is where the (PGL/PSL) layer belongs. (SO(4,4)) preserves the split quadratic form. (Spin(4,4)) preserves and lifts the spinorial structure. (PGL) forgets scale and keeps incidence. (PSL) is the determinant-normalized projective branch. They are not competing groups; they are different Erlangen readings of the same carrier.

The symmetric-space body is

[
X_K
===

SO_0(4,4)/(SO(4)\times SO(4)),
]

or the corresponding spin-cover version. This is the **Krein/generalized-metric space**: it parametrizes choices of positive and negative subspaces inside the split carrier. In generalized geometry, an (O(n,n)) structure carries the doubled pairing, and a generalized metric is exactly a reduction from (O(n,n)) to (O(n)\times O(n)). ([UAB Barcelona][3])

The conformal/projective boundary is parabolic:

[
X_P
===

SO_0(4,4)/P,
]

where (P) stabilizes a null line, an isotropic flag, or a pure-spinor line, depending on which boundary model is being used. Conformal geometry in signature ((p,q)) is modeled by (O(p+1,q+1)) on the compactified null cone; for ((p,q)=(3,3)), this gives the (O(4,4)) / (SO(4,4)) split conformal parent. ([Mathematics at LMU Munich][4]) Parabolic geometries are precisely Cartan geometries modeled on (G/P) with (P) parabolic, so this is the correct language for the projective null cone, twistor boundary, and pure-spinor boundary. ([Physics NTUA][5])

[
\boxed{
G/K
===

SO_0(4,4)/(SO4\times SO4)
}
]

for the Krein metric body,

[
\boxed{
G/P
===

SO_0(4,4)/P
}
]

for the projective-conformal/twistor boundary,

[
\boxed{
\Gamma\backslash G/K,
\qquad
\Gamma\backslash G/P
}
]

for the arithmetic automorphic quotients.

In Erlangen language, each layer is a homogeneous space (G/H). Klein geometry is exactly the study of homogeneous spaces with a transitive symmetry group and stabilizer (H). ([Wikipedia][6])

The Cartan coordinate chart should be written as a **big-cell chart**, not a global coordinate system:

[
g(u,z,\theta)
=============

\exp!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{4}u_iH_i\right)
\exp!\left(\sum_{\alpha>0}z_\alpha E_\alpha\right)
\exp!\left(\sum_A \theta^A Q_A\right).
]

Then

[
x=g(u,z,\theta)\cdot o.
]

Here (H_i) are the four split Cartan generators, (E_\alpha) are root generators, and (\theta^A) are odd Majorana/supercharge coordinates. The (u_i) are logarithmic height/scale coordinates. The (z_\alpha) are nilpotent horospherical/projective coordinates. The (\theta^A) are supercoordinates. The Weyl group folds the (u_i) into chambers, so the invariant radial data are not the raw (u_i) but the Weyl-invariant Casimir combinations.

For (D_4), the Weyl-invariant polynomial degrees are

[
2,;4,;4,;6.
]

So the spectral-shell map has the form

[
\mu
\longmapsto
\left(
C_2(\mu),
C_4^{(1)}(\mu),
C_4^{(2)}(\mu),
C_6(\mu)
\right).
]

Those are the Casimir indices of the split (D_4) shell. They are not ordinary coordinates. They are invariant labels of orbits, representations, spectral shells, and deformation strata. A recent treatment of (D_4) triality invariants explicitly lists the (W(D_4))-invariant polynomial generators in degrees (2,4,6,4). ([arXiv][7])

The modular/Möbius layer is the rank-one shadow of the same structure. Every root (\alpha) contains an (\mathfrak{sl}_2)-triple

[
(E_\alpha,F_\alpha,H_\alpha),
]

so each root direction carries a local (PSL(2,\mathbb R))-type Möbius geometry. Passing to (PSL(2,\mathbb Z)) gives the classical modular prototype. Passing to an arithmetic lattice

[
\Gamma\subset Spin(4,4)
]

gives higher-rank automorphic geometry on (\Gamma\backslash G/K) or (\Gamma\backslash G/P). Automorphic forms on reductive groups are functions with invariance under an arithmetic/rational group and finiteness conditions under compact and Lie-algebra actions; the rank-one modular forms are the simplest case of this general picture. ([swc-math.github.io][8])

След това идва Bohm–Pauli слой. Той трябва да използва `cartanDensity`, но да не живее вътре в чистия KAN слой. Причината е проста: Bohm–Pauli изисква **spinor wavefunction**, connection, Laplacian/Dirac оператор, gauge field и hydrodynamic rewrite. KAN дава мярката и координатите; Bohm–Pauli дава динамиката. Хидродинамични формулировки на Pauli/Dirac уравненията точно работят чрез амплитуда, spin density, quantum potential и fluid velocity; това е отделен динамичен слой над геометричната мярка. ([arXiv][3])

В нашия език:

[
R^2(g)
======

\operatorname{cartanDensity}(H(g)),|\Psi(g)|^2.
]

Тогава KAN-коригираният Bohm quantum potential е:

[
Q_{\mathrm{KAN}}
================

-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
\frac{\Delta_{G/K}R}{R}.
]

А Pauli-spin динамиката се записва като сертификат от вида:

[
m(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla)v
===========================

-\nabla(V+Q_{\mathrm{KAN}})
+
q(E+v\times B)
+
F_{\mathrm{spin}}
+
F_{\mathrm{Krein}}.
]

Това не означава, че (Q_{\mathrm{KAN}}) се добавя ръчно към линейното Pauli уравнение. То е **хидродинамичният readout**, който се появява след Madelung/Bohm decomposition.
