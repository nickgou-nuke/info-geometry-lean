NUCLEUS:
A positive multiplicative relative object `r` can be converted into an additive potential by `A = -log r`.

Exact compression achieved:
**relative comparison as multiplicative data -> additive displacement by negative logarithm.**

The strongest form is not “universal generator theorem.” It is:

**Negative-log rectification theorem:** under positivity and logarithmic compatibility, relative multiplicative structure becomes additive potential structure.

Only with extra structure does that potential become a dynamical generator.

INFLATIONS_TO_REMOVE:
- “Universal” is too large. The operation depends on positivity, logarithm domain, algebraic compatibility, and often commutativity or ordered/analytic structure.
- “Generator” is premature. `-log r` is first a potential or Hamiltonian-like quantity, not automatically an infinitesimal generator.
- “Exponential Breath,” “Being to Becoming,” and “Linearization of the Circle” are evocative but currently not mathematical load-bearing.
- The Lean sketch hides the hard part: `Monoid M → AddCommGroup P` is not enough for logarithm. It states an abstract homomorphism, not negative log.
- The `is_generator` field is vacuous: `evolution p 1 = evolution p 1`.
- “Sheaves of Log-Cocycles” and “Singular Rectification” are plausible future directions, but too early unless tied to exact objects: cocycles, RN derivatives, modular automorphism groups, or extended-real potentials.

AMPLIFICATION_VECTOR:
Intensify by narrowing.

The concept should move through three strict gates:

1. **Ratio Gate:** object must be positive/invertible/multiplicative.
2. **Log Gate:** `-log` converts multiplication into addition.
3. **Dynamics Gate:** only when an exponential/action/semigroup/automorphism structure exists does the additive potential generate evolution.

The archetype to preserve is not cosmic universality but **rectification**:
multiplicative relative distortion is straightened into additive potential.

Future mathematics demanded:
a typed hierarchy separating:
`RelativeRatio` -> `LogPotential` -> `FlowGenerator`,
with explicit assumptions at each transition and no theorem claiming the third from the first alone.
