## Which quantum pictures are really the same theory

Some of the standard pictures are indeed different coordinatisations of the same quantum kinematics. In geometric quantum mechanics, the space of rays in Hilbert space carries a natural Kähler structure, and Schrödinger evolution becomes Hamiltonian or symplectic flow on that projective manifold; Ashtekar and Schilling describe this formulation as equivalent to the standard algebraic formulation of quantum mechanics. That gives strong support to your broader intuition that one underlying structure can admit Hilbert, projective, and geometric presentations. citeturn5view0

The Wigner–Moyal lane is another genuine alternative presentation, not just a loose analogy. Moyal’s 1949 paper explicitly says phase-space distributions and their time-evolution equations can be used as an **alternative to the Schrödinger equation** for solving quantum-mechanical problems, and Wootters later states that for suitable systems the theory can be formulated entirely in terms of the Wigner function and phase-space functions rather than state vectors or operators. At the same time, the Wigner function is a **quasi**-probability, not an ordinary probability density, because it can take negative values. So here the “same reality, different representation” claim is basically right, but one must remember that the price of this representation is nonclassical sign structure. citeturn20view2turn13search0turn3search1

The Madelung and Takabayasi hydrodynamic picture is subtler. Takabayasi showed that by rewriting the Schrödinger equation in amplitude and phase, one obtains an ensemble of classical motions plus an internal potential, and he treated the hydrodynamical picture as formally equivalent to the statistical picture in one-body problems. But in the very same abstract he warns that if one develops this picture too literally, it can lead to a theoretical scheme “quite different” from ordinary quantum mechanics for issues beyond the equation of motion. That is an important guardrail: Madelung variables are an exact rewrite of some quantum dynamics, but the **interpretation** of those variables is not automatically equivalent to standard QM in every respect. citeturn21view1turn20view3

Bohmian mechanics sits one layer further out. Bohm’s 1952 programme and later reviews treat it as a theory of point particles with trajectories guided by the wavefunction, and modern Bohmian authors stress that it reproduces the same experimental statistics as ordinary quantum mechanics. That makes it **empirically equivalent** under the usual equilibrium assumptions, but it is not merely a change of coordinates on standard QM; it is standard QM plus additional ontology. In your language, it is not just “another chart on the same manifold,” but a thicker completion of that manifold with actual trajectories. citeturn24view0turn19search2

Stochastic mechanics is again different. Nelson’s original claim was that, in a limited framework, his construction is equivalent to non-relativistic quantum mechanics, and later expositions emphasise that the formalism is time-symmetric, using **mean forward and backward derivatives** or velocities. This is very close to your language of dual flows. But, as with Bohm, the stochastic story is not just a notation change inside the ordinary Hilbert-space formalism; it is an attempted underlying process model from which the Schrödinger equation is recovered. citeturn1search0turn22search4turn10search0

So the clean taxonomy is:

Quantum mechanics has several **exactly equivalent presentations** — Hilbert/operator, projective/Kähler, and phase-space/Wigner–Moyal among them. It also has several **empirically or dynamically related reinterpretations** — Madelung, Bohm, stochastic mechanics — that recover large parts of the same physics while changing the ontology or underlying explanatory story. Your “one information-geometric reality” slogan fits the first class strongly and the second class only with careful qualification. citeturn5view0turn20view2turn24view0turn22search4

## Where the positive and negative split really enters

The doubled or positive/negative split enters quantum theory in more than one historically distinct way, and these should not be collapsed into one undifferentiated motif.

Inside the repo, the split is algebraic and representation-theoretic. The doubled carrier carries an involution \(J\), a sign operator \(\varepsilon\), and the internal phase axis \(J\varepsilon\); the generalised metric then induces plus and minus projectors, while the inverse-kernel package compares spectral and metric projectors, their mismatch, anomaly commutators, and dilation gaps. In relativistic wave mechanics, one important positive/negative split is the **Feshbach–Villars** representation of the Klein–Gordon equation. Their 1958 review rewrites relativistic wave mechanics in a two-component, first-order form, and later summaries of the formalism note that the density appears as the difference of two positive-definite densities, naturally interpreted as the difference between positive and negative particles or charges. This is structurally quite close to a doubled/Krein way of thinking, but it is historically and mathematically a specific relativistic wave-mechanics construction, not a universal reduction of all QM pictures to one two-sheet geometry. citeturn2search0turn9search3

In stochastic mechanics, by contrast, the split is not positive/negative **energy** or positive/negative **charge**, but forward/backward **time-conditioned drift**. Modern expositions of Nelson-style mechanics describe the formalism in terms of mean forward and backward derivatives, or equivalently current and osmotic velocities. That is a very different kind of doubling. It is a split in temporal conditioning, not in charge sector or spectral sign. This distinction matters because your intuition is best when it sees a family resemblance across these constructions, but it becomes too strong if it says they are all already the same theorem. citeturn22search4turn10search0turn1search0

Penrose’s conformal and twistor language belongs to yet another layer. Twistor theory treats space-time as secondary: events are derived objects associated with holomorphic curves or incidence relations in twistor space, and Penrose’s broader conformal programme reorganises relativity around null structure and conformal infinity. Reviews of twistor theory and conformal infinity make precisely this point: conformally invariant null structure is primary, and infinity is brought into a finite geometric description through conformal compactification. This is very much in the spirit of your “same reality, different language” picture, but it is a **conformal-null encoding**, not the same object as Bohmian trajectories, Nelson drifts, or Feshbach–Villars charge splitting. citeturn27search6turn27search4turn27search2

The representations really do rhyme. But the rhyme is at the level of graded structure, causal orientation, dual decomposition, and alternative state-space geometry — not yet at the level of one universally proved identity. fileciteturn30file0L1-L1 citeturn27search6turn22search4turn2search0
