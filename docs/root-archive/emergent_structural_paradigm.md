## I. Core Mandate: Absolute Relativity of Measurement

Goutev’s Principle of Absolute Relativity of Measurement is a repository policy for formalization direction. It treats measurement as relational rather than absolute.

> There is no measurement in isolation.
> Every measurement is a comparison.
> Normalization is gauge fixing, not ontology.
> Physical state space is projective before it is normalized.

All measurement in InfoGeometry is relative measurement. A raw scalar, count, norm, energy, entropy, or amplitude is not yet a physical observable until a reference state, gauge, chart, or projective section has been specified.

### Consequences:

1.  **Prefer rays over normalized representatives.** Raw counts, scores, amplitudes, or weights are first treated as rays or relative profiles.
2.  **Prefer relative entropy over absolute entropy.**
3.  **Prefer relative potentials over absolute Hamiltonians.** The preferred primitive is a relative potential, cocycle, modular generator, or Radon–Nikodym/log-density comparison, not an isolated scalar energy.
4.  **Prefer cocycles over global homomorphisms when base-point dependence is present.** The repo privileges compositional laws: `state/reference`, `source/target`, `before/after`, `algebra/commutant`, `raw/normalized`.
5.  **Treat normalization as gauge fixing, not ontology.** Probability, density matrices, thermal states, and entropy readouts appear only after choosing a section through a projective ray.
6.  **Treat scale as a Weyl/projective coordinate unless a metric owner is supplied.** Extensive raw data is not physically final. Intensive or thermodynamic readouts are produced only after Weyl/projective gauge selection.
7.  **Treat every witness/certificate as a comparison datum until it is discharged by a local proof, Mathlib theorem, or literature-owned theorem.**

### Implementation Rule

Every bridge must expose its reference datum. Physical meaning appears only after the reference frame, normalization section, or gauge comparison has been made explicit.

Examples:
- A KMS state is relative to a reference state and modular flow.
- A projective state is relative to a chosen ray representative.
- A Weyl-gauge readout is relative to a scale/shape decomposition.
- A Fock-polarization tear is relative to two positive-frequency projectors.
- A Cantor/Fock representation bridge is relative to a normalized tilt/switch system and a cylinder/Fock embedding.

## IV. Closure-Debt Rule

A witness is not a theorem. Every certificate socket must be marked as one of:
- local-proof-owned,
- Mathlib-owned,
- literature-owned,
- research-open,
- deprecated/vacuous.

A theorem may consume a witness, but documentation must not present the witness field itself as a closed proof.

## V. Non-Authority Boundary

Goutev’s Principle guides formalization discipline. It does not by itself prove:
- a KMS theorem,
- a Type III classification,
- a Cantor/Fock infinite equivalence,
- a Hilbert–Pólya operator,
- RH,
- or a physical interpretation of every projective ray.

Those require explicit Lean owner theorems or witness-gated contexts. The repository treats absolute magnitudes as pre-observables and relative/projective/cocycle readouts as the first theorem-safe observables.
