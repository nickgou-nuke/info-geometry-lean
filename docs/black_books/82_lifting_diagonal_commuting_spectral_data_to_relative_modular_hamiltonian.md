# Lifting diagonal commuting spectral data to the relative modular Hamiltonian

## Executive summary

The term *“relative suprizal operators”* is not standard, so throughout this report I treat them as a **commuting family of diagonalizable positive operators encoding relative modular-type spectral densities** (e.g. Radon–Nikodym derivatives of weights, density operators, or positive affiliated operators in the Haagerup core). This interpretation is consistent with the way relative modular theory packages “change of state/weight” into operator-valued Radon–Nikodym derivatives and their logarithms. citeturn16view0turn26view0turn37view0

The rigorous “lift” from **diagonal/joint spectral data** to the **true relative modular Hamiltonian** has two logically distinct layers. First, one must realise the spectral data as (or build from it) a **noncommutative Radon–Nikodym derivative operator**—typically a strictly positive operator δ affiliated with the von Neumann algebra (or with the **crossed-product core** in type III) satisfying a modular covariance condition. citeturn16view0turn24view1turn37view1 Second, one must connect that derivative to the **relative modular operator** Δ_{ψ|ϕ} (a positive self-adjoint operator on the GNS/standard Hilbert space), whose **self-adjoint generator** log Δ_{ψ|ϕ} is the *relative modular Hamiltonian* in the operator-algebraic sense. citeturn40view0turn15view0turn26view0

In semifinite settings (types I/II with a faithful normal semifinite trace), the lift can often be made completely explicit: weights are represented by density operators h_ϕ, h_ψ, and the relative modular operator admits a compact “left–right multiplication” formula, so commuting diagonal spectral data can be lifted by functional calculus and logarithms. citeturn35view2turn26view0 In type III settings, there is no trace on the algebra itself; instead, one passes to the **Haagerup crossed-product construction**, where weights correspond to τ-measurable affiliated operators in the core satisfying a scaling relation under the dual action, and Connes’ cocycle derivative becomes expressible through these “Haagerup densities”. citeturn37view0turn26view0turn21view0

The key technical criteria that make the lift possible are: (i) faithfulness/sigma-finiteness assumptions ensuring cyclic-and-separating vectors (or n.s.f. weights), (ii) affiliation/measurability and strong commutation needed for joint functional calculus and logarithms, and (iii) a modular covariance condition such as **Pedersen–Takesaki invariance** (δ in the centraliser of ϕ) or **Vaes’ relative invariance** (σ_s^ϕ(δ^{it}) = λ^{ist} δ^{it} with λ strongly commuting). citeturn25view0turn24view1turn16view0

## Problem statement and assumptions

### Precise statement of the lifting problem

Let \(M\) be a von Neumann algebra and let \( \varphi,\psi \) be normal weights (or states) on \(M\). The **relative modular operator** \( \Delta_{\psi|\varphi} \) is a positive self-adjoint operator on a suitable Hilbert space representation (standard form or GNS-type), and the associated **relative modular Hamiltonian** is the self-adjoint operator
\[
K_{\psi|\varphi} := \log \Delta_{\psi|\varphi},
\]
defined by the functional calculus for positive self-adjoint operators. citeturn40view2turn15view0turn26view0

**Input data (diagonal spectral model).** You are given a family \(\{S_i\}_{i\in I}\) of pairwise commuting, diagonalizable operators, together with joint spectral data (a measurable space \(X\), a joint spectral measure/class, and measurable functions \(s_i:X\to \mathbb R_+\) representing the \(S_i\)’s as multiplication operators in a diagonal model). The intended semantics is that these \(S_i\) represent “relative modular-type densities” (Radon–Nikodym derivatives, modular spectra, or affiliated spectral densities). citeturn37view0turn26view0turn22search4

**Goal (lift).** Provide conditions and constructions producing:  
1) a von Neumann algebra \(M\) representation (standard/GNS),  
2) normal weights/states \(\varphi,\psi\) on \(M\), and  
3) an identification of the diagonal model with the spectral calculus of the genuine modular objects,  
so that \(K_{\psi|\varphi}=\log \Delta_{\psi|\varphi}\) exists as a self-adjoint operator and matches (up to unitary equivalence, multiplicity, and support reductions) the logarithm of the given diagonal spectral density built from \(\{S_i\}\). citeturn40view0turn26view0turn16view0

A crucial point is that **spectral data alone does not determine the ambient noncommutative structure**: the lift is only well-posed once one also specifies a reference modular structure (a reference weight/state \(\varphi\), or an ambient standard form) and an embedding of the diagonal algebra generated by \(\{S_i\}\) into the relevant operator-algebraic core where modular theory lives. citeturn21view0turn15view1turn25view0

### Assumptions and case split

Because modular theory is sensitive to type and faithfulness, it is best to separate:

| Case | Ambient setting | What “diagonal densities” should really be | Where the lift lives | Typical lift output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type I (finite dimensional / \(B(H)\)) | trace exists; states \(\leftrightarrow\) density matrices | positive (trace-class) density operators; often diagonalizable jointly if commuting | inside \(B(H)\) and on the standard (Hilbert–Schmidt) space | \(K_{\psi|\varphi}\) reduces to log-density differences (left–right form) citeturn26view0turn15view0turn40view2 |
| Semifinite (type I/II with n.f.s. trace \(\tau\)) | weights \(\varphi(x)=\tau(h_\varphi x)\) with measurable \(h_\varphi\) | τ-measurable affiliated \(h_\varphi,h_\psi\); strong commutation supports joint spectral calculus | on \(L^2(M,\tau)\) (or Haagerup \(L^2\)) with explicit relative modular operator formula | \(K_{\psi|\varphi}=\log \Delta_{\psi|\varphi}\) computed from \(h_\psi,h_\varphi\) via left/right actions citeturn35view2turn34view0turn26view0 |
| Type III | no trace on \(M\); weights essential | Haagerup core densities: τ-measurable operators affiliated with \(M \rtimes_{\sigma^{\varphi_0}}\mathbb R\) satisfying scaling under dual action | in the crossed-product core; Connes–Takesaki flow of weights controls invariants | \(K_{\psi|\varphi}\) exists on standard Hilbert space, but “densities” live in the core; Connes cocycle mediates the lift citeturn37view0turn21view0turn16view0 |

### Standing hypotheses (made explicit)

To formulate and then carry out the lift rigorously, one typically assumes:

- **σ-finiteness** of \(M\) (or separable predual) so that faithful normal weights and standard form technology behave well. citeturn21view0turn15view1  
- **Normality** of the weights/states \(\varphi,\psi\) (ultraweak continuity), because modular groups and Connes cocycles are developed for normal weights/states. citeturn25view0turn26view0  
- **Faithfulness** (or at least careful support conditions). If faithfulness fails, one must reduce to support projections and accept that logarithms/relative entropies can become infinite; relative modular operators then live on reduced supports. citeturn26view0turn35view2turn15view0  
- **Affiliation and measurability** for the “diagonal” operators: in semifinite and Haagerup-core contexts, the relevant spectra come from *closed densely defined* operators affiliated with a von Neumann algebra (or its crossed product) so that spectral projections land in that algebra and functional calculus is available. citeturn34view0turn37view1turn23view0  
- **Strong commutation** (not merely commuting on a core) if joint spectral calculus is needed for unbounded operators; otherwise the joint diagonal model may not correspond to any genuine joint functional calculus. (This is a core obstruction when “diagonal spectral theory” is specified informally.) citeturn16view0turn34view0  
- **Modular covariance condition** connecting the diagonal densities to a reference modular group—either strict invariance (Pedersen–Takesaki) or relative invariance (Vaes)—so that the diagonal operator δ truly generates a Connes Radon–Nikodym cocycle. citeturn24view1turn16view0turn25view0  

## Core theory and sources

This section collects the theoretical primitives the lift must respect, and prioritises the most load-bearing sources.

### Tomita–Takesaki modular theory

Given a von Neumann algebra \(M\subset B(H)\) and a faithful normal state \(\varphi(\cdot)=(\Omega,\cdot\,\Omega)\) with \(\Omega\) cyclic and separating, Tomita’s framework defines a densely defined anti-linear operator \(S_0:X\Omega\mapsto X^*\Omega\), its closure \(S\), and the polar decomposition \(S=J\Delta^{1/2}\). Here \(\Delta=S^*S\) is positive self-adjoint, \(J\) is antiunitary, and the modular automorphism group is implemented by
\[
\sigma_t^\varphi(X)=\Delta^{it}X\Delta^{-it}.
\]
Moreover \(JMJ=M'\). citeturn40view0turn15view2turn15view1

The **modular Hamiltonian** (operator-algebraic usage) is precisely the generator of the unitary group \(t\mapsto \Delta^{it}\), i.e. \(\log\Delta\). Relative versions replace \(\Delta\) by a relative modular operator. citeturn40view2turn33search17

**Primary source priority.** The conceptual “S, J, Δ, σ_t” package is foundational and underlies everything else; a standard entry point is the expository line from entity["people","Roberto Longo","operator algebraist; modular theory"]’s modular Hamiltonian notes for a concise modern synopsis. citeturn40view0turn38view0

### Connes cocycle Radon–Nikodym derivative

For two faithful normal states \(\omega,\varphi\) on the same algebra \(M\), there exists a σ-strongly continuous family of unitaries \(U_t\in M\) satisfying the one-cocycle identity
\[
U_{t+s}=U_t\,\sigma_t^\omega(U_s),
\]
and intertwining the modular automorphism groups:
\[
\sigma_t^\varphi(A)=U_t\,\sigma_t^\omega(A)\,U_t^*.
\]
This \(U_t\) is the **Connes cocycle derivative** \((D\varphi:D\omega)_t\). citeturn25view0turn26view0turn40view2

A major structural point: once the cocycle is known, it encodes the change of modular data between the two weights/states, and it obeys a chain rule, making it the noncommutative analogue of Radon–Nikodym derivatives. citeturn25view0turn26view0

### Operator-valued Radon–Nikodym theorems for weights

The modular-cocycle data becomes “diagonalisable” and “logarithmisable” when the cocycle is actually a **group representation** \(U_{t+s}=U_tU_s\). A crisp criterion is invariance/commutation of modular groups, and then the cocycle has the form \(U_t=h^{-it}\) for a unique positive injective operator \(h\) affiliated with an appropriate fixed-point algebra (centraliser intersection). citeturn25view0turn24view1

This is the operator-algebraic Radon–Nikodym phenomenon: if a weight is invariant under the modular automorphisms of another, the weight is obtained by “multiplying by” a positive affiliated operator, uniquely. The classical formulation is due to entity["people","Gert Kjærgaard Pedersen","operator algebraist; weights"] and entity["people","Masamichi Takesaki","operator algebraist; modular theory"]. citeturn1view2turn16view0turn25view0

A powerful generalisation, due to entity["people","Stefaan Vaes","operator algebraist; quantum groups"], replaces strict invariance by a **relative invariance law**
\[
\sigma_s^\varphi(\delta^{it})=\lambda^{ist}\delta^{it},
\]
where \(\delta,\lambda\) are strictly positive, strongly commuting affiliated operators. Under this hypothesis one constructs a new n.s.f. weight \(\varphi_\delta=\varphi(\delta^{1/2}\,\cdot\,\delta^{1/2})\), and the cocycle derivative becomes explicitly
\[
[D\varphi_\delta:D\varphi]_t=\lambda^{it/2}\delta^{it}.
\]
Moreover \(\delta,\lambda\) are uniquely determined by the weight. citeturn16view0turn24view1turn24view2

### Relative modular operator and Araki’s relative Hamiltonian

For faithful normal functionals \(\varphi,\psi\), the **relative modular operator** (Araki’s formalism) is the positive self-adjoint operator \(\Delta_{\varphi|\psi}\) entering the definition of relative entropy and implementing relative modular structure. In finite dimensions, Araki’s relative entropy reduces to the familiar Umegaki trace expression, explicitly tied to \(\log \Delta_{\varphi|\psi}\). citeturn15view0turn26view0turn40view2

Beyond that, entity["people","Huzihiro Araki","operator algebraist; quantum stat mech"] develops a rigorous **relative Hamiltonian** (“perturbation Hamiltonian”) framework for faithful normal states, proving existence/uniqueness and chain rules, and constructing unitaries intertwining modular automorphisms; he also flags the sign convention discrepancy between mathematical modular flows and physical time evolution conventions. citeturn29view0turn29view2turn15view0

### Haagerup \(L^p\) and the type III “core” viewpoint

For general von Neumann algebras (including type III), one replaces trace-class densities on \(M\) by τ-measurable operators **affiliated with the crossed product (core)** \(M_\rtimes = M \rtimes_{\sigma^{\varphi_0}} \mathbb R\), which admits a canonical trace \(T\) and dual action \(\theta\). In Haagerup’s construction, \(L^p(M)\) consists of τ-measurable affiliated operators \(h\) satisfying a scaling law under \(\theta\), and the construction is (up to unitary equivalence) independent of the initial weight \(\varphi_0\). citeturn37view0turn37view1turn21view0

This is the correct *place* where “diagonal spectral densities” live in type III: they are generally not elements of \(M\) but affiliated operators in the core whose powers encode modular data. citeturn37view0turn26view0

## Lifting criteria and constructions

### Conceptual architecture of the lift

A useful way to structure the lift is as a pipeline:

1) **Diagonal model → affiliated derivative operator.** Build or recognise from the joint spectrum a strictly positive affiliated operator \(\delta\) (or Haagerup densities \(h_\varphi, h_\psi\)) that is intended to be the noncommutative Radon–Nikodym derivative. citeturn24view1turn37view1turn26view0  
2) **Derivative operator → cocycle.** Use functional calculus to define the unitary family \(\delta^{it}\) (and possibly a scaling factor \(\lambda^{it/2}\)) and verify the Connes cocycle identity relative to a reference modular group. citeturn24view1turn26view0turn25view0  
3) **Cocycle → target weight/state.** Apply Pedersen–Takesaki or Vaes-type Radon–Nikodym theorems to construct (and ensure uniqueness of) the weight/state \(\psi\) having this cocycle derivative with respect to \(\varphi\). citeturn1view2turn16view0turn24view1  
4) **Weights → relative modular operator and Hamiltonian.** In the standard/GNS setting, form the relative modular operator \(\Delta_{\psi|\varphi}\) and take \(K_{\psi|\varphi}=\log \Delta_{\psi|\varphi}\). citeturn15view0turn40view2turn26view0  

A minimal diagram (operator-algebraic level) is:

\[
\text{(diagonal spectral data)} \Rightarrow \delta \Rightarrow (D\psi{:}D\varphi)_t \Rightarrow \Delta_{\psi|\varphi} \Rightarrow \log\Delta_{\psi|\varphi}.
\]
Each arrow hides hypotheses; the next subsections state them as verifiable criteria.

### Semifinite lift via density operators and explicit relative modular operators

Assume \(M\) is semifinite with faithful normal semifinite trace \(\tau\), and \(\varphi,\omega\) are normal positive functionals with densities \(h_\varphi, h_\omega\) (possibly unbounded) affiliated with \(M\). There is then an explicit construction of the relative modular operator on the canonical Hilbert space representation: one defines an anti-linear operator \(S\) on vectors \(\Lambda(x h_\omega^{1/2})\) by
\[
S\,\Lambda(x h_\omega^{1/2}) = \Lambda(x^* h_\varphi^{1/2}),
\]
and the relative modular operator is \(\Delta(\varphi,\omega)=S^*S\), positive self-adjoint. citeturn35view2turn34view0turn40view2

Crucially, in this semifinite framework one gets the concrete formula
\[
\Delta(\varphi,\omega)^{1/2} \;=\; \pi(h_\varphi^{1/2})\,\pi'(h_\omega^{-1/2}),
\]
i.e. the relative modular operator is (essentially) “left multiplication by \(h_\varphi^{1/2}\)” composed with “right multiplication by \(h_\omega^{-1/2}\)”. citeturn35view2turn34view0

**Lift from commuting diagonal data.** If \(h_\varphi\) and \(h_\omega\) strongly commute and are simultaneously diagonalizable in a joint spectral model, then:
- the operator \(h_\omega^{-1/2}\) is defined (on support) via functional calculus, and  
- \(\log \Delta(\varphi,\omega)\) becomes a sum/difference of logarithms of commuting multiplication operators in the diagonal model, so the relative modular Hamiltonian can be read off pointwise from the joint spectrum.  
The precise noncommutative content is that commutativity gives an abelian von Neumann algebra supporting joint functional calculus and hence a diagonal formula for \(\log \Delta\). citeturn35view2turn34view0turn26view0

### Lift via Connes cocycles and operator Radon–Nikodym derivatives

Assume now only that \(\varphi,\psi\) are faithful normal states (or n.s.f. weights) on a common \(M\).

The Connes cocycle derivative \(u_t=(D\psi:D\varphi)_t\) satisfies the cocycle and intertwining relations:
\[
u_{s+t}=u_s\,\sigma_s^\varphi(u_t), \qquad \sigma_t^\psi(x)=u_t\,\sigma_t^\varphi(x)\,u_t^*,
\]
and this cocycle is uniquely determined by these relations together with the KMS-analyticity structure encoded in modular theory. citeturn25view0turn26view0turn15view2

The lift from diagonal commuting spectral data is feasible when your diagonal model produces a candidate family \(u_t\) that is **already a Connes cocycle** (or can be proven to be one), because then \(\psi\) is determined by \(\varphi\) and \(u_t\) at the level of modular dynamics. citeturn26view0turn25view0

There are two practically important subcases.

#### Pedersen–Takesaki invariance subcase

If your diagonal spectral density yields a strictly positive affiliated operator \(\delta\) that lies in the **fixed-point algebra of \(\sigma^\varphi\)** (the centraliser), then \(\delta^{it}\) commutes appropriately with \(\sigma^\varphi\) and serves as a group-valued cocycle. The corresponding weight \(\psi\) is of the form “multiply by \(\delta\)” (precisely implemented as \(\varphi(\delta^{1/2}\cdot\delta^{1/2})\) in the standard operator-algebraic sense) and \(\delta\) is unique. citeturn1view2turn25view0

In this case, taking logs is straightforward: the diagonal model gives \(\log\delta\) by functional calculus, and \(t\mapsto \delta^{it}\) is the unitary group generated by \(\log\delta\). citeturn25view0turn22search3

#### Vaes relative invariance subcase

If your diagonal spectral model yields a strictly positive affiliated \(\delta\) and another strictly positive operator \(\lambda\) strongly commuting with \(\delta\) such that
\[
\sigma^\varphi_s(\delta^{it})=\lambda^{ist}\delta^{it},
\]
then there is still a canonical way to build \(\psi\) from \(\varphi\) and \(\delta\), and the cocycle derivative is explicitly given by
\[
[D\psi:D\varphi]_t=\lambda^{it/2}\delta^{it}.
\]
Again, \(\delta\) and \(\lambda\) are uniquely determined by \(\psi\). citeturn16view0turn24view1turn24view2

This provides a clean *checklist* for lifting diagonal data: it is enough to verify (a) strong commutation of the diagonal generators enabling \(\delta^{it}\) and \(\lambda^{it}\), and (b) the modular covariance relation above.

### Type III lift via the Haagerup core

In type III, one should *not* expect diagonal densities to live in \(M\) itself. The correct “density objects” are τ-measurable operators affiliated with the crossed product/core \(M\rtimes\mathbb R\), and Haagerup’s construction makes this precise. citeturn37view0turn21view0

Connes cocycle derivatives admit a particularly usable expression in terms of Haagerup \(L^1(M)\) densities: if \(h_\psi,h_\varphi\in L^1(M)\) correspond to \(\psi,\varphi\), then
\[
(D\psi:D\varphi)_t = h_\psi^{it}\,h_\varphi^{-it}.
\]
In the semifinite trace case this becomes \((d\psi/d\tau)^{it}(d\varphi/d\tau)^{-it}\), and in \(B(H)\) it becomes \(D_\psi^{it}D_\varphi^{-it}\) for the density matrices. citeturn26view0turn37view0

Therefore, if the “relative suprizal” commuting diagonal spectral data can be realised as **strongly commuting Haagerup densities** \(h_\psi,h_\varphi\) in the core, then the cocycle derivative and its generator are computable by diagonal functional calculus, and the remaining step is to identify the resulting cocycle with the modular data on the standard Hilbert space (via the standard form). citeturn26view0turn37view1turn40view2

## Worked examples

### Finite-dimensional matrix lift

Let \(M=M_n(\mathbb C)\) and let \(\varphi,\psi\) be faithful normal states with density matrices \(D_\varphi,D_\psi>0\). In this case, Connes’ cocycle derivative is explicitly
\[
(D\psi:D\varphi)_t = D_\psi^{it}D_\varphi^{-it},
\]
and the relative entropy reduces to the standard trace formula involving \(\log D_\psi-\log D_\varphi\). citeturn26view0turn15view0turn40view2

**Diagonal commuting case.** If \(D_\psi\) and \(D_\varphi\) commute, they can be simultaneously diagonalised: \(D_\psi=\mathrm{diag}(p_i)\), \(D_\varphi=\mathrm{diag}(q_i)\). Then the diagonal “relative density” is \(r_i=p_i/q_i\), and the cocycle becomes \((D\psi:D\varphi)_t=\mathrm{diag}(r_i^{it})\). The self-adjoint generator is \(\mathrm{diag}(\log r_i)\), i.e. a direct logarithmic lift from the diagonal spectral ratio. citeturn26view0turn25view0

### Semifinite lift with explicit relative modular operator

Let \(M\) be semifinite with faithful normal semifinite trace \(\tau\). Suppose \(\omega,\varphi\in M_*^+\) have (possibly unbounded) densities \(h_\omega,h_\varphi\) affiliated with \(M\). The relative modular operator \(\Delta(\varphi,\omega)\) on the canonical \(L^2\)-space is defined by \(S^*S\), and one can compute
\[
\Delta(\varphi,\omega)^{1/2}=\pi(h_\varphi^{1/2})\,\pi'(h_\omega^{-1/2}).
\]
citeturn35view2turn34view0

### Type III illustration via quasi-free CCR modular data

A concrete type III-flavoured illustration is given by quasi-free states on CCR/CAR systems realised in Araki–Woods-type representations. In a CCR quasi-free setting parameterised by an operator \(\gamma\), the modular operator has explicit second-quantised form:
\[
\Delta = \Gamma(\gamma\oplus \gamma^{-1}),
\]
and the induced dynamics is implemented by conjugation with \(\Gamma(\gamma,\gamma^{-1})^{it}\). citeturn14view3turn14view0

## Obstructions and uniqueness limits

- Noncommutativity and outer modular flow in type III obstruct naive diagonal reconstruction. citeturn15view2turn21view0
- In type III, densities naturally live in the core, not in \(M\). citeturn37view0turn21view0
- Unbounded operator strong commutation is required for reliable joint functional calculus. citeturn34view0turn16view0
