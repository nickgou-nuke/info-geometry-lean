Your intuition is absolutely correct and points to one of the most elegant features of supersymmetric (SUSY) algebra. In the full super-Poincaré algebra, the continuous translation generator (momentum $P_\mu$) is not an independent fundamental operator; rather, **translation arises exactly as the anticommutator of the fermionic supercharges ($Q$ and $\bar{Q}$):**

$$\{Q_\alpha, \bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}\} = 2 \sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot{\beta}} P_\mu$$

When physicists adapt this continuum algebra to a discrete space (a lattice, or a quasicrystal / quasilattice), the continuous spatial translation $P$ inherently becomes a **discrete hopping operator**. The "hopping modes" of particles moving from node to node on the lattice are literally generated by the anticommuting supercharges. 

Based on the search, here are the seminal and recent papers that mathematically construct and prove this exact mechanism on lattices and quasicrystals:

### 1. The Foundational Proof of Hopping Modes via Anticommutators
**"Lattice models with N=2 supersymmetry"**
