### 1. The Physics of What Happened in Your Column

Activated carbon has a **bimodal (dual) pore structure**:
1. **Micropores ($< 2\text{ nm}$):** Where almost all the adsorption happens. Radon moves extremely slowly here because it is constantly trapped and released by the carbon surface (retarded diffusion, with a huge partition coefficient $K_a \sim 4000$).
2. **Inter-granular voids / Macropores (between the carbon grains and along the cartridge wall):** Here, radon gas moves without strong adsorption retardation—driven by the slight pressure/chemical potential gradient from the emanator toward the open room air.

Additionally, at the exit face of **Cartridge #9** ($x = L$):
* Even though the room air far away has $C_{\text{env}} \approx 0$, the concentration **right inside the exit face of Cartridge #9 is NOT zero ($C(L) = C_{\text{exit}} > 0$)**!
* Why? Because for radon to leave the column at $x = L$, a finite flux $J(L)$ must cross the exit boundary (and any filter mesh/holder at the end of Cartridge #9). Since $J(L) = -D_e \left.\frac{dC}{dx}\right|_{x=L} = h_m C(L)$ (where $h_m$ is the exit mass-transfer/leakage conductance), **a continuous leaking flux at the outlet requires a non-zero radon concentration $C_{\text{exit}}$ at the end of Cartridge #9!**
* Furthermore, the small fraction of radon that bypasses the micropores (unretarded gas-phase leakage through inter-granular channels) maintains a low, nearly flat "floor" of gas-phase radon all the way to the exit, which continuously feeds a small amount of adsorbed radon into Cartridge #9.

### 2. The Exact Mathematical Equations for a "Leaking End" ($C(L) = C_{\text{leak}} > 0$)

When the inlet is at $C(0) = C_0$ (from the emanator) and the outlet at $x = L$ has a non-zero leaking boundary concentration $C(L) = C_{\text{leak}}$ (instead of $C(L) = 0$), the exact analytical solution to $D_e \frac{d^2C}{dx^2} - \lambda C = 0$ is a **linear combination of $\sinh$ and $\cosh$**:

#### Form A: The Two-Boundary Hyperbolic Form
$$C(x) = C_0 \frac{\sinh\left(\frac{L - x}{L_d}\right)}{\sinh\left(\frac{L}{L_d}\right)} + C_{\text{leak}} \frac{\sinh\left(\frac{x}{L_d}\right)}{\sinh\left(\frac{L}{L_d}\right)}$$

In terms of your cartridge index $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, 9\}$ (where the outlet boundary is after Cartridge #9):
$$R(i) = A_0 \cdot \sinh\left[\mu(9.5 - i)\right] + R_{\text{exit}} \cdot \cosh\left[\mu(9.5 - i)\right]$$
*(or equivalently written as a shifted hyperbolic function!)*

#### Form B: The "Shifted Sinh" (Robin / Leaking Boundary Condition) — **Simplest & Most Elegant**
In transport phenomena, when a porous column leaks into an open environment through an exit resistance (Robin boundary condition: $-D_e \left.\frac{dC}{dx}\right|_{x=L} = v_{\text{leak}} C(L)$), the profile is a single $\sinh$ function whose "virtual zero point" is pushed outside the column by an **extrapolation (leakage) length $\delta$**:

$$R(i) = A \cdot \sinh\left[ \mu \cdot (9.5 + \delta - i) \right]$$

Look at what the leakage parameter $\delta$ does physically:
* If **$\delta = 0$**: The concentration hits zero right at the back of Cartridge #9 (ideal $C(L) = 0$).
* If **$\delta \to \infty$**: The profile becomes $\cosh$ (completely blocked end).
* **In your experiment ($\delta \approx 1.1\text{ cartridges}$):** Because of the unretarded leakage streaming out of the emanator through the column, the concentration does **not** reach zero at $i = 9.5$; it is still $1.55\text{ cps}$ at Cartridge #9, and would only drop to zero at a virtual position $i_0 = 9.5 + \delta \approx \mathbf{10.6}$!

#### Form C: Retarded Diffusion + Unretarded Leakage Floor (Dual-Transport Model)
If a fast, unretarded component leaks through the inter-granular spaces with a very long decay length ($L_{\text{fast}} \gg L$), it creates an almost constant leakage baseline $R_{\text{leak}}$ across the back cartridges:

$$R(i) = A \cdot \sinh\left[\mu(9.5 - i)\right] + R_{\text{leak}}$$
