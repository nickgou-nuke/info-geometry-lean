Yes. For the projective interpretation, do **not** start with the matrix algebra (M_{2^N}(\mathbb R)). Start with the abstract split/Krein space and its Clifford module.

Let (E) be an (N)-dimensional real state space, and form the doubled space

[
K := E\oplus E^* .
]

It has the canonical split bilinear form

[
\langle u+\alpha,;v+\beta\rangle
================================

\frac12\big(\alpha(v)+\beta(u)\big),
\qquad
u,v\in E,\ \alpha,\beta\in E^* .
]

So (K) is a finite-dimensional Krein space of signature ((N,N)). This is the abstract doubled indefinite metric space; no matrix representation is needed. The corresponding Clifford algebra is

[
Cl(K)=Cl(N,N),
]

with relation

[
zz'+z'z=2\langle z,z'\rangle .
]

This (T\oplus T^*)-type split metric and the associated spinor realization by exterior forms are exactly the algebraic model used in generalized geometry: (T\oplus T^*) carries a natural split-signature metric, and its spin bundle is modeled by differential forms. ([Annals of Mathematics][1])

The key (Z_2)-graded Clifford module is

[
S:=\Lambda^\bullet E^*
======================

S^+\oplus S^-,
]

where

[
S^+=\Lambda^{\mathrm{even}}E^*,
\qquad
S^-=\Lambda^{\mathrm{odd}}E^* .
]

The Clifford action is the creation-annihilation action

[
(u+\alpha)\cdot \rho
====================

\iota_u\rho+\alpha\wedge\rho ,
\qquad
\rho\in \Lambda^\bullet E^* .
]

Here

[
\iota_u^2=0,
\qquad
(\alpha\wedge)^2=0,
]

so the elementary null Clifford generators are genuinely nilpotent. Their anticommutator is

[
\iota_u(\alpha\wedge)+(\alpha\wedge)\iota_u
===========================================

\alpha(u)\operatorname{id}.
]

Thus the two null halves (E) and (E^*) are the annihilation/creation polarizations of the split Clifford system:

[
E^2=0,
\qquad
(E^*)^2=0,
\qquad
E,E^*+E^*E\sim \langle E,E^*\rangle .
]

The (Z_2)-chirality is then not imposed externally. It is the parity of the exterior degree:

[
\chi\rho=(-1)^{\deg\rho}\rho .
]

Odd Clifford elements exchange chirality,

[
K\cdot S^\pm\subset S^\mp,
]

while the even Clifford algebra preserves chirality,

[
Cl^0(K)\cdot S^\pm\subset S^\pm .
]

Therefore

[
\operatorname{Spin}(K)\subset Cl^0(K)
]

acts separately on the two projective chiral spinor spaces

[
\mathbb P(S^+),
\qquad
\mathbb P(S^-).
]

Projectively, a spinor state is not a normalized vector (\rho). It is a ray

[
[\rho]\in \mathbb P(S),
\qquad
\rho\sim \lambda\rho,\quad \lambda\neq0 .
]

So “unnormalized states” are the correct objects. The zero spinor is excluded; scalar magnitude is gauge.

For a nonzero spinor (\rho), define its Clifford annihilator

[
\operatorname{Ann}(\rho)
:=
{z\in K:\ z\cdot\rho=0}.
]

Because

[
z\cdot w\cdot \rho+w\cdot z\cdot\rho
====================================

2\langle z,w\rangle\rho,
]

if (z,w\in\operatorname{Ann}(\rho)), then

[
\langle z,w\rangle=0 .
]

So (\operatorname{Ann}(\rho)) is automatically isotropic. A spinor is **pure** when

[
\dim \operatorname{Ann}(\rho)=N,
]

i.e. when its annihilator is a maximal isotropic subspace of (K). This is Cartan’s pure-spinor projective geometry: pure spinors correspond to maximal totally isotropic subspaces. Trautman states this in the same annihilator language: a spinor is pure when its associated null space is maximal among totally isotropic subspaces.
