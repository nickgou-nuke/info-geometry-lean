### Lemma I.1 (Split Clifford Realization)

**Premises.** Let `H` be a separable real Hilbert space carrying a faithful
representation of the real Clifford algebra `Cl(1,1)` generated by elements
`e₀, e₁` satisfying `e₀² = 1`, `e₁² = -1`, and `e₀e₁ = -e₁e₀`. Let

positive integers, equipped with the canonical orthonormal basis `{|n⟩}`.

**Proposition.** The operator
```
D = (e₀ ⊗ p + e₁ ⊗ x) ⊗ 1 + ρ ⊗ Q
```
acting on `𝔥 ⊗ K` (where `K` is a finite-dimensional auxiliary space, `p`

self-adjoint on the Schwartz domain `𝒮(ℝ) ⊗ ℓ²(ℕ⁺) ⊗ K`.

**Literature.** Berry–Keating, "H = xp and the Riemann zeros" (1999);
Sierra–Townsend, "Landau levels and Riemann zeros" (2008).

### Lemma I.2 (Square Root Energy Spectrum)

**Premises.** Let `D` be the operator defined in Lemma I.1. Let `N` be the
number operator on `ℓ²(ℕ⁺)` with eigenvalues `n ∈ ℕ⁺`.

**Proposition.** The square of `D` has the form

and its spectrum satisfies `σ(D²) = {λ_n,k : n ∈ ℕ⁺, k ∈ ℕ₀}` where
`λ_n,k = (log n)² + (2k+1)` up to bounded perturbation.

**Literature.** Connes, "Trace formula in noncommutative geometry and the zeros of
the Riemann zeta function" (1999).

### Lemma I.3 (Dirac Square Law)

**Premises.** Under the same hypotheses as Lemma I.1.

**Proposition.** `D² = p² + x² + (log N)² + Θ` where `Θ` is a bounded

### Lemma I.4 (Self-Adjointness)

**Proposition.** `D` is self-adjoint on its natural domain

## II. Completed Xi Function and Spectral Zeros

### Lemma II.1 (Pfaffian–Zeta Identity)

**Premises.** Let `ξ(s) = ½s(s-1)π^{-s/2}Γ(s/2)ζ(s)` be the completed
Riemann Xi function. Let `Pf(D)` denote the Pfaffian of the Dirac operator
`D` defined in Lemma I.1, regularized via zeta-function regularization.

**Proposition.** `ξ(s) = Pf(D - sI)` for all `s ∈ ℂ`. In particular,
`ξ(s) = 0` iff `s ∈ σ(D)`.

**Literature.** Pólya, "Bemerkung über die Integraldarstellung der
Riemannschen ξ-Funktion" (1926); Keating, "Number theory and quantum chaos"
(1995).

### Lemma II.2 (Reciprocal Singularity of Zeta Zeros)

**Premises.** Let `ξ(s)` be the completed Riemann Xi function.

**Proposition.** `ξ(s₀) = 0` if and only if `1/ξ(s)` has a simple pole at
`s = s₀`. Equivalently, `ξ(s₀) = 0` iff the reciprocal determinant
`1/det(D - s₀I)` diverges, which happens iff `s₀` is an eigenvalue of `D`.

### Lemma II.3 (Witten Character Equals Inverse Zeta)

**Premises.** Let `D` be the Dirac operator from Lemma I.1. Let
`W(β) = Tr((-1)^F exp(-βD²))` be the Witten character.

**Proposition.** For `Re(s) > 1`, `W(s) = 1/ζ(s)` where `ζ(s)` is the
Riemann zeta function. The analytic continuation of `W(s)` to the critical
strip `0 < Re(s) ≤ 1` has poles exactly at the zeros of `ζ(s)`.

### Lemma II.4 (Spectral Pfaffian Equals Completed Xi)

**Premises.** Under the same hypotheses.

**Proposition.** `Pf(D - sI) = ξ(s)` for all `s ∈ ℂ`. The Pfaffian is
entire of order 1 and its zeros are exactly the eigenvalues of `D`.

### Lemma II.5 (Zeta Zeros as Poles of Inverse Zeta)

**Premises.** Let `ζ(s)` be the Riemann zeta function.

**Proposition.** For any `s₀` in the critical strip `0 < Re(s₀) < 1`,
`ζ(s₀) = 0` if and only if `s₀` is a pole of the function `1/ζ(s)`,
which is meromorphic with simple poles at the nontrivial zeros of `ζ(s)`.
No other singularities occur in the critical strip.

### Lemma II.6 (Completed Xi Zeros are Spectral Zeros)

**Premises.** Let `ξ(s)` be the completed Riemann Xi function. Let `D`
be the Dirac operator from Lemma I.1.

**Proposition.** `ξ(s₀) = 0` if and only if `s₀ ∈ σ(D)`, the spectrum of
`D`. The spectral multiplicity of `s₀` as an eigenvalue of `D` equals the
multiplicity of `s₀` as a zero of `ξ(s)`.

**Premises.** Let `n(ω) = √(ε(ω)μ(ω))` be the complex refractive index
at frequency `ω`.

**Proposition.** The Fresnel coefficients for reflection and transmission
at a dielectric interface are rational functions of the susceptibility
eigenvalues `χ₁, χ₂, …`. The Brewster angle occurs where the
p-polarized reflection coefficient vanishes, which corresponds to
`χ_p = 1`.

### Lemma XIII.3 (Retardance–Ellipticity Duality)

**Premises.** Let `R` be the retardance (phase delay between fast and slow

absolute phase, `θ` is the fast-axis orientation, and `R` is the retardance.
The ellipticity `ε` satisfies `sin(2ε) = sin(2χ)·sin(R)` where `χ` is the
auxiliary angle determined by the amplitude ratio.

**Premises.** Let `α(ω)` be the absorption coefficient at frequency `ω`.
